# Chapter 2: Blood Dawn and the One-Bar Anomaly

The first pale rays of dawn filtered through the heavy morning mist, slowly illuminating the wild landscape that had been swallowed by pitch darkness throughout the night.

Thame woke abruptly from a fitful, shallow sleep, having spent the entire night in terrified vigilance, listening to every vibration beyond the cab.

Primal survival instinct guided his right hand out of Uncle Det's duck-down sleeping bag, immediately resting his palm against the checkered grip of the .38 Special revolver beside him on the sleeper berth.

His fingers were stiff and numb from the biting mountain cold that had seeped past the rubber weatherstripping.

He gently pushed open the cab door. The metallic screech of the latch echoed loudly into the eerie, primeval stillness.

Plumes of white vapor billowed from his mouth and nostrils with every ragged breath.

A freezing morning gale whipped down from the mountain pass. Heavy mist drifted languidly across the valley floor, saturated with the raw, chilling humidity typical of deep mountain wilderness.

What greeted his eyes was not the wooded shoulder of a highway, nor the crushed guardrails he expected.

Before him stretched a vast natural basin of coarse alpine grasses and scattered limestone outcrops.

Brittle yellow turf was soaked in heavy morning dew, interspersed with massive karst boulders strewn chaotically across the ground.

Beyond lay an unblemished expanse of ancient two-needle pines and primeval mixed forests so dense it was chilling. Straight, towering trunks pierced the sky, extending toward jagged limestone mountain ridges that encircled the valley in all directions, their sheer cliffs puncturing rolling banks of white cloud.

No power poles.

No paved asphalt.

Not even a single cart track or trace of human civilization.

Thame stepped down from the cab, circling the 25-ton rig. The orange corrugated steel walls of the container were splattered with thick, dried mud.

His eyes caught three deep gouges carved clean through the industrial enamel and into the bare steel plates of the container wall.

Crusted blood and coarse, bristly gray-brown fur clung to the steel burrs. The musky, pungent stench of a colossal predator still lingered faintly in the air.

The harrowing brush with death from the night before flashed back with visceral clarity... The sedan-sized beast that had clawed the metal was terrifyingly real.

Thame knelt and crawled underneath the chassis to inspect the mechanical damage with an engineer's eye.

The sight made the automotive engineering graduate's stomach drop:

Both front leaf spring packs of 5160 spring steel were sheared clean off their hanger shackles.

The tandem rear leaf springs were warped and cracked. The main heavy-duty driveshaft was twisted into an S-curve.

The front right radial tire and rear duals were shredded, steel belts protruding through torn rubber, the rims sunk nearly half a meter into thick, sucking clay.

"Wrecked beyond repair... completely immobilized," Thame muttered with a grim chuckle at his absurd misfortune.

He still believed he had merely skidded off the shoulder of the northern mountain highway. The immediate worry of a fresh college graduate lingered in his mind—penalties for late parcel deliveries and negative star ratings on the driver app.

Yet with the truck trapped in deep mud, his first priority was establishing contact with the outside world for emergency rescue.

Thame climbed the steel ladder bolted to the container flank, scaling the four-meter height to the roof to scan for the highway and search for cell signal.

Then, an impossible miracle occurred: the screen that had displayed "No Service" all night suddenly flashed, and a solid white signal bar flickered to life... **One bar! (2G / E)**

"Cell service?! There's a signal on the roof!" Thame gasped in disbelief.

With trembling thumbs, he frantically dialed emergency 191 and local rescue services.

Yet the device chimed with a blunt error tone, severing the call with the prompt: *'Call Failed'*. The single flickering 2G bar was far too unstable to sustain a voice channel.

He attempted opening messaging apps and satellite maps, but the loading spinners circled endlessly; the bandwidth was far too sluggish for modern data streams.

However, a global plain-text community forum application (Global Community Forum) began caching text strings line by agonizing line!

Thame wasted no time. He tapped into the Emergency & Search-and-Rescue board and created an urgent thread.

Title: **[EMERGENCY] 10-wheel logistics truck rammed off northern mountain pass yesterday afternoon. Stranded in uncharted karst gorge, cannot make calls, 1 bar of internet. Please alert rescue teams!**

He rapidly typed out his truck model, license plate, and the mountain highway corridor, explaining that his rig was wrecked in a deep karst gorge and threatened by an enormous beast.

Less than two minutes later, the first response flashed onto the screen from `@ChiangMai_Rescue_Vol`:

*"A logistics truck was indeed reported missing on the pass! Last night our search teams swept on foot and deployed thermal drones along the ravine at the skid marks. But down below there's only dense brush and a sheer blind cliff. There is NO sign of your truck or wreckage anywhere! The tire tracks vanished into thin air! Where are you? Send GPS coordinates immediately!"*

Thame typed back frantically: GPS was spinning indefinitely, unable to lock onto any satellites. Forum members urgently advised him to take panoramic photos of his surroundings to cross-reference with topographic maps.

Using his phone camera, Thame snapped wide panoramic shots of the towering, needle-sharp limestone spires, the ancient pine canopy, and the three deep claw gouges on the container door. He compressed the images into small files of dozens of kilobytes and uploaded them through the sluggish 2G connection.

When the photos loaded, the thread exploded!

`@ChiangMai_Rescue_Vol:` *"Wait... where on earth are you?! We pulled high-res satellite maps from the Forestry Department along the entire pass corridor. There is NOT A SINGLE karst valley with this topography anywhere in the region!"*

`@Paleo_Geology:` *"Where was this taken?! Look at those limestone spires—they are razor-sharp, completely un-weathered by tens of thousands of years of rain and wind erosion! Spires like that do not exist in the modern world. And that pine forest... it's textbook terminal Pleistocene flora!"*

`@VikingBushcraft:` *"And look at those claw marks on the steel door! Gouges deep enough to slice through sheet steel, with that paw spread—that belongs to an ice age apex predator weighing well over a ton. Are you even in the modern era?!"*

`@TrollMaster_99:` *"LMAO! 10/10 CGI editing! Claiming you dropped into the Stone Age? Who's gonna believe that!"*

While users argued intensely, the fragile 2G bar on the screen suddenly flickered out, returning to "No Service", while the battery warning flashed below 20%.

Thame stood petrified on the roof, his breath seizing at the words... *"Not in the modern era?!"*

Yet... that was merely the beginning of the true nightmare.

Day one faded into pitch-black night... Day two... Day three...

No rescue helicopters appeared. No sirens wailed. No human silhouette emerged on the ridges.

The 1.5 liters of warm tea in Uncle Det's thermos had been rationed to the last drop by the morning of Day 2.

The few mint candies discovered in the glove box were long dissolved in his stomach. The cab contained not a single crumb of food.

Agonizing hunger gnawed at his intestines with burning cramps. His stomach growled violently, his lips cracked and bled, and his throat turned as parched as desert sand.

The freezing gale howling down from the mountain pass grew fiercer each day, biting through his thin shirt until he shivered uncontrollably, his fingers and toes losing sensation from the damp cold.

Night temperatures plummeted until frost coated the tall grass in sparkling white ice.

At night, the guttural roars and heavy, ground-shaking footfalls of primeval beasts circled the steel container, forcing him to curl inside the sleeping bag gripping the .38 revolver in sheer terror.

Venturing into the primeval jungle on foot was certain suicide.

Trapped in the deep mountain gorge for days, he was completely out of options... Starvation, dehydration, and hypothermia were steadily draining his life away.

Finally, primal survival instinct triumphed over professional ethics and moral constraints.

"I'm so sorry, customers... I have no choice left. If I don't open these, I will die right here," Thame whispered through bitter tears of desperation.

Gripping Uncle Det's heavy bowie knife, he climbed down from the cab, marched to the rear of the truck, severed the lead customs seal with a hard blow, and heaved open the heavy steel doors.

He had to ransack the cargo hold to find food to eat and survival gear to withstand the deadly cold!

The scent of cardboard and bubble wrap from over 3,500 parcels rushed out. Thame scanned the shipping labels frantically in the dim light.

The first box he tore open was `#TH-994201`: a **Heavy-Duty Goose Down Parka** rated for -20°C.

He slipped it on immediately. The thick down trapped his body heat instantly, cutting the freezing wind and stopping hypothermia in its tracks.

The second box was `#TH-118492`: a **1095 High-Carbon Steel Bushcraft Knife**.

With a four-millimeter spine, a razor-sharp Scandinavian grind, and olive-drab micarta scales, it included a molded Kydex sheath and an integrated ferrocerium rod. He clipped it to his left hip immediately.

Thame used the new blade to slice open the third parcel:

At last, he struck salvation against starvation: `#TH-204192`, an emergency survival ration pack containing **Four 500g High-Energy Compressed Ration Bars** and **Two 1-Liter Bottles of Natural Mineral Water**!

Thame ripped open the vacuum foil, ravenously biting into the dense biscuit.

Rich, sweet nutty malt flavors and concentrated calories surged into his bloodstream, reviving his failing strength.

He uncapped a water bottle, gulping down the crisp, ice-cold mineral water until half the bottle was drained, banishing the torturous thirst that had haunted him for days.

Tears of relief streamed down his cheeks as miraculous energy surged back into his exhausted body.

The final parcel he opened was `#TH-773019`: a rugged **20,000 mAh Solar Power Bank**, fully charged at 100%.

Thame plugged his dead smartphone into the power bank via the braided nylon cable.

With the 1095 knife at his hip and the down parka hood drawn over his head, he climbed the container ladder back to the roof to catch the sunlight and survey the valley.

The smartphone screen flared to life, and atop the roof, the single bar of 2G flickered back into existence.

Before he could touch the screen, Thame's gaze swept across the drifting sea of mist toward the limestone ridge two hundred meters away—and froze.

Perched upon the jagged rocky outcrop, three human figures stood tall against the biting wind.

They were clad in coarse, charcoal-gray animal hides. In their hands, they gripped long thrusting spears tipped with glistening black flint, and tall recurve bows fashioned from seasoned wood.

The figures crouched slightly, assessing the "giant steel beast" below.

Three pairs of predatory eyes beneath tangled, braided hair were locked unblinkingly onto the solitary young man standing atop the roof!

The sight of living Stone Age hunters, coupled with the forum's warnings about a late Pleistocene ecosystem days before, crashed into Thame's mind with devastating force...

He hadn't merely crashed off a mountain road... He was truly stranded in the Stone Age, ten thousand years in the past!
