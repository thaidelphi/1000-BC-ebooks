# Chapter 2: Blood Dawn and the One-Bar Anomaly

The pale golden glimmer of first dawn bled through the suffocating mist, slowly unveiling a wilderness that had been shrouded in black terror throughout the night.

Thame stirred from a fitful, restless sleep spent straining his ears against every shadow. Driven by raw survival instinct, his right hand reached out from the goose-down sleeping bag to touch the checkered walnut grip of the .38 Special revolver resting right beside him on the bunk.

His fingers were stiff and numb from the biting sub-zero frost that had seeped past the cab's rubber gaskets during the long vigil. He flexed his joints to drive the chill away, scanning the cramped cabin with sharp wariness.

He pushed the driver's door open.

The hinge pins groaned with a sharp metallic screech that echoed unnervingly across the silent, primordial valley.

Breath plumed from his lips in thick, billowing clouds.

Outside, the mercury hovered at six degrees below zero Celsius. A sharp northern gale whipped fine rime crystals against his face, stinging raw skin.

What met his eyes was not the asphalt ribbon of Route 9 or the steel guardrails of modern civilization.

Before him stretched an infinite, rolling expanse of tundra.

Brittle dwarf grasses were rimed in silver frost, punctuated by colossal glacial erratics—boulders the size of cottages plowed into erratic heaps by ancient ice sheets.

Far on the northern horizon, jagged limestone peaks tore into the heavens, their jagged crests crowned in eternal blue ice.

No power lines.

No paved roads.

Not even the faint rut of a cart or the smoke of a modern chimney.

Thame stepped down from the cab, his steel-toed boots crunching loudly on the frozen, mud-locked permafrost.

He dropped to his knees and crawled beneath the massive undercarriage of the ten-wheeler to verify the mechanical reality with his own eyes under the morning light.

What he saw struck a fatal blow to the automotive engineer within him.

Both front leaf spring packs, forged from 5160 spring steel, were sheared clean off at the spring hangers.

The tandem rear bogie springs were bowed and twisted; the heavy main driveshaft was warped into a ruined, useless S-curve.

The right steer tire and both rear duals on the drive axle were lacerated down to their steel radial belts. The massive steel wheel rims were buried nearly half a meter into solid, frozen mud.

"Completely wrecked... beyond any field repair in this lifetime," Thame muttered, a hollow, self-deprecating smile curling his lips.

Though he had driven this ten-wheeler in place of Uncle Det countless times and knew every bolt and hydraulic line of its chassis, damage of this magnitude—sheared leaf springs and a twisted driveshaft stranded in a primeval wilderness without a garage—was beyond the salvaging power of any engineer.

He had simply agreed to take the wheel for his uncle to cover rent while waiting out engineering job callbacks. And now, the twenty-five-ton rig he was piloting had tumbled across ten thousand years of spacetime.

"If a delivery is ten thousand years overdue... how many millions in penalty fees does the courier app charge?"

"My driver rating is probably negative ten million by now."

The unforgiving geology around him delivered a cold slap of reality:

In a primeval world ten thousand years before Christ, with no gravel roads or tracks, a twenty-five-ton highway truck couldn't travel a single kilometer even if magically repaired.

The hummocky tundra, hidden glacial crevices, and peat bogs were death traps for heavy wheeled vehicles.

This corrugated steel cargo box was his sole permanent stronghold—an immovable fortress.

Thame stood and rounded the rig. His gaze caught the deep, violent gouges carved into the steel side of the cargo box.

Three jagged furrows tore through the industrial primer, exposing bright, raw steel.

Matted tufts of coarse, grey-brown fur and dried blackish blood clung to the shredded metal edges.

The rank, musk-heavy stench of the cave bear still lingered faintly in the sub-zero air—a grim reminder that the apex titan of the Ice Age was very real, and very close.

A violent shiver racked his body as the bitter wind cut clean through his cotton t-shirt and thin jacket. Thame hurried to the rear of the truck and climbed into the container hold.

Inside the corrugated steel hold, dim morning light seeped between the heavy doors across over 3,500 sealed parcels stacked from floor to ceiling.

All his life, Thame had been raised with strict working-class integrity. As a trucker entrusted with cargo, every single box represented someone's hard-earned money and anticipation. Under any ordinary circumstance, he would never dream of tampering with customer deliveries.

Yet now... he was truly out of options. Every other human path was gone.

Outside, the temperature hovered near minus ten degrees. The glacial wind sliced through his thin clothes; his lips were turning blue, and his fingertips were trembling violently from early hypothermia. Without serious arctic insulation, he would freeze to death before noon—and a dead courier delivers nothing to anyone.

Staring at the courier waybills, a knot of acute guilt tightened in his chest. He lowered his head toward the boxes, offering a silent, solemn apology to the unknown recipient.

"Forgive me, whoever you are... I am completely out of options. If I don't open this, I freeze to death right here. If I ever make it back to civilization, I swear I'll repay every single cent."

Driven by sheer biological survival and the absolute absence of any alternative, Thame took a steadying breath and drew his box cutter, carefully scoring the clear packing tape.

The first package he targeted was parcel `#TH-994201`: an elongated corrugated box weighing roughly 2.4 kilograms. The shipping label indicated an outdoor gear distribution center in the northern highlands.

He sliced the clear packaging tape and flipped open the cardboard flaps.

The crisp scent of brand-new synthetic fabric greeted him.

Inside lay a **Premium Goose Down Parka** in charcoal gray.

The outer shell was heavy-duty, windproof, ripstop nylon, complete with an insulated, fleece-lined storm hood. The manufacturer's tag guaranteed thermal protection down to -20°C.

"Thank god..." Thame breathed a ragged sigh of relief. He slipped the parka on immediately.

Warmth enveloped his shivering torso like an embrace. The lofty goose down trapped his core heat, steadily driving away the bone-deep chill.

The second box was parcel `#TH-118492`: a compact rectangular parcel weighing roughly 0.8 kilograms.

Cutting it open revealed a sleek black box containing a **1095 High-Carbon Steel Bushcraft Fixed-Blade Knife**.

The spine was a stout four millimeters thick with a non-reflective black oxide coating. The five-inch drop-point blade had a razor-sharp Scandinavian grind, fitted with olive-drab micarta handle scales that locked securely into his palm.

It came with a molded Kydex sheath featuring a heavy belt clip and an integrated ferrocerium fire striker.

Thame drew the blade; the edge caught the dim light like a surgical scalpel. He clipped the sheath to his left hip immediately—his primary field tool and close-quarters backup.

The third parcel was `#TH-773019`: a gray bubble-lined mailer weighing 0.5 kilograms.

Tearing it open revealed an ultra-rugged **20,000 mAh Solar Power Bank**.

Encased in thick, shockproof high-visibility orange-and-black rubber, its back housed an integrated monocrystalline solar panel, an emergency LED floodlight, and braided multi-port charging cables.

He tapped the battery status button. Four solid blue LEDs flared to life—100% full factory charge.

"Enough juice for four or five phone charges. And as long as the sun shines, it regenerates."

Thame pulled his ruggedized smartphone from inside his jacket and connected the braided USB-C cable. Battery sat at 85%.

He grabbed the power bank and climbed the steel service ladder fixed to the side of the container, scaling the four-meter height to reach the roof. He needed to angle the solar panel toward the sun and get an unobstructed 360-degree vantage point.

The gale on the container roof was savage, flapping the tail of his down parka violently.

As morning mist burned off in the rising sun, the landscape opened up, revealing a colossal glacial fault line slicing across the western horizon.

As Thame set the power bank flat on the ribbed steel roof, an impossible vibration buzzed against his palm.

*BZZZZ... BZZZZ...*

The smartphone in his hand vibrated.

The top-right corner of the screen, which had read "No Service" all night, suddenly pulsed with a tiny, flickering signal bar.

The grey icon turned solid white... **One bar! (2G / E)**

Thame's eyes dilated in sheer shock. His heart thumped against his ribs like a war drum.

"Cell service?! How the hell is there a roaming signal on top of a truck in 10,000 BC?!"

With trembling thumbs, he unlocked the display.

Mainstream messaging apps failed to connect—the bandwidth was far too sluggish for modern media streams or VoIP protocols.

However, a global plain-text community forum application (Global Community Forum) began caching text strings line by agonizing line!

Thame didn't hesitate. He tapped into the Emergency & Wilderness Survival sub-forum and hit *New Thread*.

Title: **[EMERGENCY] 10-wheel parcel truck crashed through a dimensional rift into the middle of nowhere. Heavy fog, suspension destroyed, wheels shattered, sub-zero cold. A beast the size of a sedan just clawed the rig. Someone please help!**

Body:
*"I was driving an afternoon logistics haul on Route 9 when an unnatural black storm swallowed the pass. Woke up in an endless tundra with massive boulders and glacier walls. No roads, no power lines, sub-zero temps. Rig is completely immobilized. Just survived an attack by an enormous bear that dented the steel doors. I only have one flickering bar of 2G on top of the truck roof. Anyone with geological coordinate knowledge or survival expertise, please advise!"*

With shaking fingers, he pressed *Post*.

The loading spinner hung for ten agonizing seconds before the green banner popped up: *Post Successful.*

Thame stood shivering in the biting wind, his chest tight with unbearable suspense.

Thirty seconds later, the first notification pinged onto the screen.

`@TrollMaster_99:`
`"LMAOOOO! What survival game is this promoting, bro? Or is this an ARG marketing campaign for an indie horror flick? 10/10 creative writing! Transported to the stone age but you’ve still got bandwidth to post on Reddit? Drop the server IP, I’m logging in right now!"`

`@VikingBushcraft:`
`"If this isn't a sick joke... a predator the size of a sedan matches Ursus spelaeus—the Ice Age Cave Bear. DO NOT leave that vehicle. The sweat and pheromones of modern humans are an irresistible scent marker for Pleistocene apex predators!"`

Thame exhaled a ragged breath, half-exasperated, half-terrified. "Idiots... I’m not playing an online game! I’m actually trapped here!"

Before he could type a furious reply, his eyes flicked up past the bezel of the phone toward the limestone ridge across the draw.

He froze.

Roughly two hundred meters away, perched on a sheer rocky outcrop through the drifting mist...

Three human silhouettes stood motionless against the gray sky.

They were clad in thick, ragged charcoal-gray animal hides. In their hands, they gripped long thrusting spears tipped with glistening black obsidian points, and tall, recurve hunting bows fashioned from seasoned sinew and timber.

Three pairs of predatory eyes, staring beneath tangled, braided hair, were locked unblinkingly onto the colossal steel monolith—and the solitary young man standing atop its roof.

Thame's breath caught in his throat. His right hand instinctively dropped to the hilt of the 1095 bushcraft knife at his hip.

The first contact between two civilizations separated by ten thousand years had just begun.
