# Chapter 9: The Law of the Rations and the Brass Clue

The deep, mournful howling of dire wolves echoed across the jagged limestone cliffs in the moonless dark.

A sharp northern wind whipped through the mountain cleft, slamming into the corrugated aluminum flanks of the container with a hollow, menacing rumble.

Freezing mist clung to the truck’s windshield and coated the gravel with brittle frost as the temperature plummeted to 7–8°C.

Though the apocalyptic deep freeze of the Younger Dryas was still four to five months away, this late autumn chill bit ruthlessly into human bone.

The lingering scent of pine smoke and boiled venison fat from the titanium pot still drifted through the icy air.

To the prehistoric predators lurking in the valley, that rich scent was an unmistakable beacon drawing them toward Horseshoe Gorge.

Inside the perimeter of Fortress Alpha, quiet panic spread among the thirty souls of the Mountain Deer Clan.

Children trembled against their mothers' chests, while elders huddled close to small embers beneath the truck’s heavy chassis.

Hunters gripped their fire-hardened stone spears, dark eyes scanning the pitch-black shadows beyond the narrows with raw dread.

Piles of gnawed deer bones, slippery fat trimmings, and fresh entrails lay strewn haphazardly near the massive tires.

The stench of raw blood was carried far down the canyon draft, advertising an effortless feast for apex carnivores.

Thame stood by the rear container ladder, arms folded across his chest, his breath billowing in white clouds under knitted brows.

As a logistics officer, he understood with absolute clarity: without strict camp hygiene and waste protocols, this haven would become an abattoir.

"We cannot let this stand... If we don't bring order to this camp right now, none of us survive the night."

Turning back inside the pitch-black cargo hold, Thame swept his LED flashlight over the stacked cardboard boxes.

His trained eyes scanned the waybill labels until they locked onto medium-duty corrugated carton `#TH-192844`.

The shipping manifest indicated a sanitary wholesale distributor delivering industrial hygiene and camping gear to a remote mining camp, gross weight 3.8 kilograms.

Thame sliced through the heavy packing tape with his SK5 utility blade and folded back the sturdy flaps.

Packed snugly inside were five large packs of hospital-grade antibacterial wet wipes infused with green apple fragrance, eighty sheets per pack.

Beneath them lay a heavy contractor roll of jumbo 30x40-inch black garbage bags, fifty bags in total, manufactured from puncture-resistant polyethylene.

And tucked along the bottom was a heavy-duty 4x6-meter olive-drab nylon flysheet, PU-coated for full waterproofing, complete with twelve reinforced brass grommets and paracord tie-downs.

Thame ran his fingers across the ripstop diamond weave with immense relief; here was an impenetrable barrier against freezing dew and gale-force drafts.

He grabbed a pack of wet wipes, flipped open the snap cap, and pulled free a thick, moist white towelette.

The crisp aroma of green apples and clean antiseptic bloomed through the container, instantly dispelling the stifling odors of damp cargo.

Stepping down into the firelight by the front bumper, Thame approached Kuran and Moga, who stood watch with furrowed brows.

Thame extended the moist wipe toward the clan chieftain. Kuran recoiled instinctively before gingerly leaning forward to inhale.

The old hunter's eyes widened in sheer disbelief; the sweet, refreshing moisture prompted him to reach out and touch the cloth with trembling fingertips.

Thame demonstrated by scrubbing grease, venison fat, and black pine soot from his own palms, leaving his skin immaculate within seconds.

Kuran passed the miraculous fabric to Moga. The old shaman took a deep breath, collapsed to his knees, and pressed his forehead to the dirt, whispering reverently of "the sacred spirit-cleansing leaf."

A wave of astonished murmurs rippled through the gathered women and children. Little Tara toddled forward with round, pleading eyes.

Thame pulled another fragrant wipe and handed it to her gently.

The little girl cradled the soft sheet against her chubby cheeks, giggling with pure delight as she inhaled the sweet apple scent, breaking the camp's suffocating dread.

Thame gave her head a warm pat before hardening his resolve back to the immediate crisis.

He snapped open a massive black garbage bag, gesturing forcefully for the hunters to gather every bone scrap, bloodied clump of dirt, and piece of offal.

Every scrap of butcher waste was shoved deep into the thick plastic, and Thame tied the neck off in a tight, airtight knot.

The sickening stench that had hung over the perimeter vanished as if swallowed by magic behind the heavy polyethylene seal.

Next, Thame picked up a blackened ember from the fire and began sketching upon a flattened cardboard flap, giving birth to humanity's first tactical map.

Using deliberate gestures and a bird’s-eye perspective, he explained the geography of Horseshoe Gorge to Kuran and his war party.

Prehistoric hunters, whose vision of the world had always been confined to ground level, stared in stunned silence as their towering canyon was captured upon a single sheet.

At the heart of the map, directly over the sketch of the ten-wheeler, Thame inscribed bold English letters with charcoal: **"FORTRESS ALPHA."**

In a prehistoric world ten thousand years in the past without a single named territory, this was the very first name he bestowed—both a solemn anchor for human survival and a strategic tactical coordinate to communicate with the online war room on 2G. Kuran and the clan hunters gazed upon the foreign glyphs in hushed, reverent awe, treating them as the citadel's sacred sigil.

Thame established absolute camp laws: all refuse and deep latrine trenches were banished eighty meters to the south, downwind of the canyon.

No food scraps or human waste would be tolerated near the sleeping quarters or freshwater springs; any violation meant immediate forfeiture of food rations.

Kuran nodded vigorously in total comprehension, barking orders for two strong hunters to take the trench trowel and utility knife to dig the latrine trench at once.

Meanwhile, Thame strung the 4x6-meter waterproof flysheet between the container’s roof rim and natural limestone pillars using tight paracord hitches.

The olive-drab canopy created an angled windbreak, sealing the vulnerable draft beneath the chassis and forming a dry shelter against the freezing night.

Elderly women and nursing mothers quickly nestled beneath the nylon roof, shielded from the biting wind and gathering frost.

As Thame made his final safety inspection around the tires, El-ya limped toward him, using a sturdy wooden spear as a crutch.

Her fever had broken and her dark eyes burned with intense focus. Without a word, she opened her rough, calloused right hand before him.

Lying in her palm was no flaked flint spearhead or carved mammoth bone.

It was a gleaming, cylindrical brass casing, roughly two centimeters long, bearing a distinct firing pin indentation on its primer cup.

Thame’s heart skipped a violent beat; cold dread flooded his veins.

"A 9mm Parabellum spent casing..." Thame breathed, his voice barely a whisper against the wind.

He picked up the metal cylinder, inspecting the crisp headstamp stamped into the brass rim: *9mm Luger WIN*.

Bringing it to his nose, he detected the unmistakable, lingering acrid tang of smokeless cordite residue—fired within recent days.

It had not come from his .38 Special revolver, nor old trucker Wan’s meager belongings.

El-ya used urgent hand gestures, pointing toward the deep glacial fault line to the southwest.

She pantomimed tracking a fresh deer carcass whose shoulder bone had been shattered clean through by an explosive puncture, completely devoid of stone arrowheads.

And this golden metal relic had been discovered on a boulder beside the kill, bearing strange scorch marks of unnatural sulfur.

The horrifying truth struck Thame like a hammer: another person from the modern world was stranded in this epoch, and they were armed with modern military firearms.

Even worse, that armed stranger might already be leading the cannibalistic horde terrorizing the adjacent valleys.

With an overwhelming sense of urgency, Thame climbed the metal rungs to the container roof into the howling night air.

It was nearly 22:00. The single 2G signal bar blinked awake in the corner of his smartphone screen.

Shivering on the exposed roof as the wind sliced through his jacket, Thame tapped frantically into the modern forum thread:

`"Found a spent 9mm Luger brass casing in the primeval valley... Someone else crossed over with firearms!"`

`"Night temp dropping fast (7–8°C). Does anyone know how to track months or predict when deep winter hits in 10,000 BC?"`

He hit submit, and responses began flashing onto the screen within minutes.

`@BioArch_Oxford:`
`"If you found 9mm brass, there are modern firearms active in your sector. Stay on extreme alert! It could be escaped convicts or armed mercenaries!"`

`@AstroNerd_Munich:`
`"Regarding seasons: tomorrow at local solar noon, drive a straight stick vertically into flat ground (a Gnomon) and measure shadow length versus stick height."`
`"Post the shadow ratio and Polaris angle; I will calculate solar declination to estimate how many months until the Younger Dryas deep freeze hits!"`

`@Tactical_Prepper:`
`"9mm has an effective combat range of 50 meters. With only a .38 revolver, never engage in open ground! Fight from within your steel container fortress!"`

`@CombatMedic_US:`
`"Stay buttoned up, kid! Stew scent carries for miles downwind. Keep 24-hour watches; Ice Age dire wolves will never abandon an easy meal!"`

Right on cue, the 2G connection severed, plunging the screen into darkness and the mountain pass into silence.

Thame climbed down into the encampment, fingers brushing against the cold steel of the .38 revolver in his coat... forty-three rounds remained his final bastion of hope.

White frost now formed a sparkling crust across the gravel, the earth crunching brittle beneath his boots as late autumn heralded the coming winter.

Fortress Alpha settled into quiet vigilance, all carrion odors safely sealed inside heavy plastic, while controlled embers flickered beneath the steel chassis.

Yet... forty meters out in the ink-black shadows beyond the narrows, among the craggy boulders.

Pairs of glowing emerald and amber eyes began to emerge from the dark, one by one.

The dire wolves had ceased their howling entirely, now stalking forward toward the camp in absolute, predatory silence.
