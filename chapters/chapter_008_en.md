# Chapter 8: The Titanium Pot and Extinct Flora

Pale gold light from the dawn of the fifth day filtered through the frozen mist, painting the towering limestone crags in a vibrant wash of amber and bronze.

Glittering hoarfrost encrusted the windshield and corrugated container roof of the ten-wheeler, the early morning temperature hovering at a brisk six to seven degrees Celsius.

Amidst the biting autumn chill, the thirty members of the Cliff Deer Tribe stirred from the warmest, safest sleep they had known in years.

They gathered around small, crackling campfires nestled beneath the massive steel chassis.

Women began stretching and scraping damp pelts to air-dry, while hunters methodically inspected knapped flint points and fire-hardened spear hafts.

Thame descended from the cab, breath pluming into thick white clouds against the frosty air.

Clad in his goose-down parka with the bushcraft knife sheathed at his hip, he mounted the rear ladder into the dark cargo box to check on El-ya.

The young huntress was already sitting up against a wall of cardboard boxes, healthy color returning to her sun-browned cheeks, her amber eyes bright and lucid.

The angry crimson swelling on her right thigh had subsided remarkably. The drainage wound was clean, devoid of foul putrefaction or necrotic stench.

Thame dabbed sterile cotton soaked in Betadine around the margins before packing a fresh strip of sterile gauze into the tract.

He then poured clean boiled water into the thermos cap and handed her **one 500mg Amoxicillin capsule**.

This was her scheduled morning dose—the third consecutive dose to maintain continuous bactericidal inhibition against residual anaerobic colonies.

El-ya swallowed the medication without hesitation. She offered Thame a faint, grateful smile, her calloused fingers lightly resting against the back of his hand in profound acknowledgment.

Yet outside the container, an urgent logistical crisis loomed: food rations for thirty human beings.

While the hunters had managed to haul a frozen hindquarter of an ancient wild alpine deer from their abandoned caves, the meat was tough, stringy, and frozen solid.

They were attempting to boil thick chunks in a crude, porous earthenware pot riddled with hairline fissures.

Water dripped incessantly into the embers, sending up plumes of acrid ash, and two hours of vigorous stoking had barely warmed the dense flesh.

"Cooking like that will burn through our firewood, waste fresh water, and nobody eats before noon," Thame muttered, shaking his head.

He retreated into the dark cavern of the cargo container, beam sweeping across the camping and outdoor survival section.

His light snagged on a compact cardboard carton marked with barcode `#TH-710492`.

The thermal waybill read clearly: *Ultralight Titanium Outdoor Cookware & High-Energy Camping Rations, Weight: 2.1 kg.*

Thame flicked open his SK5 utility blade and sliced through the tape.

The unpacked contents brought a low whistle of appreciation to his lips:

- **Ultralight Pure Grade-1 Titanium 3-Piece Cookware Set**, including a 1.6-liter hanging bail-handle pot, an 800ml nesting companion pot/mug, and a dual-purpose frypan lid. Weighing less than 300 grams total, yet capable of enduring direct open flames exceeding 1,600°C without warping or degrading.
- **High-Energy Dark Chocolate Survival Bars** with wild nuts and organic honey, vacuum-sealed in heavy-duty foil (5 large bars).
- **Concentrated Beef Bouillon Cubes**, boxed set of 12 foil-wrapped cubes.

"Titanium cookware... every ultralight backpacker's holy grail," Thame chuckled softly under his breath.

Grabbing the titanium pots, one bouillon cube, a chocolate bar, and the bag of pure sea salt `#TH-881205`, he vaulted down to the frozen ground.

Beckoning two young hunters, Thame led them a hundred and fifty meters across the scree to the **White Stone Stream**.

The glacial meltwater flowed crystalline and pure over beds of cold dolomite limestone.

Thame filled the 1.6-liter titanium pot to the brim with pristine water before returning to the camp.

Setting three limestone rocks into a stable tripod beside the truck, he set the titanium pot directly over the blazing spruce embers.

With the SK5 box cutter, he cubed the tough venison into bite-sized morsels with effortless precision.

As the water began to murmur, he dropped the meat into the pot.

He followed with one cube of concentrated beef bouillon and a small pinch of pure sea salt—roughly five grams.

The rapid thermal conductivity of the paper-thin titanium alloy brought the liquid to a rolling, violent boil at a speed that left the natives wide-eyed.

Within minutes, an intoxicating, heavenly aroma of rich simmered beef, aromatic herbs, spices, and savory mineral steam rolled across the frozen encampment.

All thirty members of the Cliff Deer Tribe froze mid-motion, their nostrils flaring as they inhaled scents never before experienced in the history of their lineage.

Jaws went slack. Eyes dilated with unadulterated astonishment as collective mouths watered uncontrollably.

Once the venison had simmered to tender perfection, Thame swung the pot off the fire and ladled steaming broth and tender meat into the 800ml titanium mug.

He carried it up to El-ya inside the truck first.

The huntress took a tentative sip of the piping-hot broth. The savory richness and dense caloric punch bloomed across her tongue, bringing an immediate flush of vital energy to her cheeks.

Next, Thame ladled hearty stew into the rough-hewn wooden bowl of little Tara, who sat waiting patiently beside the rear tandem wheel.

Tara cradled the hot bowl with both hands, taking a deep, greedy gulp of the hot broth. The rich symphony of fats, herbs, and savory salt exploded across his palate.

The boy chewed the tender meat in delirious wonder, his cheeks puffed full as glorious warmth broke a fine sheen of sweat across his forehead.

Thame wasn't done yet. He peeled back the foil on the high-energy dark chocolate bar, snapped off a small square, and held it out to the child.

"Try this, little man. Sweets from another world."

Tara took the dark brown cube into his mouth with hesitant suspicion.

The instant it melted across his tongue, the deep, bittersweet cocoa, crushed roasted nuts, and pure wildflower honey delivered a staggering blast of dopamine straight to his Paleolithic brain.

Tara’s dark eyes went wide as saucers, his jaw dropping in stunned disbelief.

He leapt to his feet, dancing and jumping around the fire in a burst of uncontainable, ecstatic joy such as the boy had never known!

Old shaman Moga crept forward, timidly reaching out to touch the cooling titanium pot.

To the elder, this silver vessel was lighter than an eagle's feather, harder than granite, and impervious to the fiercest infernos without cracking or melting.

It was nothing less than the "Sacred Silver Cauldron of the Sky."

While the clan feasted in jubilant camaraderie, Thame took the opportunity to climb the ladder to the container roof to check his cellular connection.

At 10:15 AM, the sub-arctic wind briefly subsided. The phone screen blinked: 1 bar of 2G signal.

Thame snapped a panoramic photo of the steaming titanium pot centered over the campfire, flanked by the eating clan members and the stark, breathtaking limestone backdrop.

Right beside the cooking stones, clinging tenaciously to the frost-shattered limestone fissure, grew a low, prostrate dwarf alpine shrub with thick, scalloped, leathery leaves and distinctive spiraling, feathery dried seed styles.

Thame uploaded the image to the international survival forum thread with a succinct bulletin:

*`[Day 5 Update] El-ya’s septic fever has broken and she’s eating venison stew. Cellulitis is receding cleanly. Unboxed a titanium camping pot today and cooked broth for the entire clan. Deep gratitude to our combat medic for the surgical and antibiotic protocol.`*

The active thread detonated instantly with thousands of notifications.

Yet a reply from a verified Oxford paleobotanist seized the entire community's attention:

`@BioArch_Oxford:`
`"EVERYONE STOP LOOKING AT THE TITANIUM POT! Zoom in on the scree rocks directly behind the fire!"`

`"That prostrate dwarf shrub with scalloped, rugose leathery leaves and twisted feathery styles... that is Dryas octopetala (Mountain Avens) in its un-hybridized, pristine Late Pleistocene morphotype!"`

`"It is the exact geological index fossil plant after which the Younger Dryas cold reversal was named! That specific ancestral variety has been extinct at that latitude and altitude for nearly ten thousand years!"`

`"Furthermore, that razor-sharp karst tower ridge behind them... our geological satellite GIS databases show NO limestone ridge of that morphology currently existing anywhere on Earth! Where on this planet did you take this photograph?!"`

A prominent forum troll chimed in, visibly unsettled:

`@TrollMaster_99:`
`"Bro... OP’s CGI rendering is getting scary now. Procedurally generated prehistoric weeds in Unreal Engine 5.3? But honestly, that venison stew looks ridiculously fire. Drop the recipe!"`

`@AstroNerd_Munich:`
`"I’ve run solar inclination calculations based on the shadows cast by the pot and truck rim... The sun's declination precisely matches a late-autumn solar trajectory at roughly 10,200 BCE in the Northern highland latitude!"`

`@CombatMedic_US:`
`"Glad the kid made it, soldier! Make damn sure she finishes the full 7-day Amoxicillin course! But watch your six—the smell of boiling meat in sub-zero air will carry for miles downwind!"`

The combat medic’s tactical warning resonated with a jolt in Thame’s chest.

Before he could type a response, the fragile 2G signal sputtered and died.

Thame descended the ladder rapidly, the medic’s warning echoing ominously in his mind.

And that warning materialized far faster than anyone could have anticipated.

At twilight on the fifth day, as freezing shadows swallowed the canyon once more.

A deep, bone-rattling, chest-thumping howl echoed from the dark pine forests to the north.

*AWOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!*

The cry was no common timber wolf. It was massive, resonant, and thunderous as a mythical beast.

Within ten seconds, dozens of answering howls erupted in horrifying harmony, ringing from every ridge and gorge, encircling the limestone sanctuary on all flanks!

Kuran snatched up his spear, veins bulging. El-ya scrambled for her recurve bow. Every member of the clan froze in paralyzing terror.

The scent of surgical blood and rich venison stew had summoned the most apex nightmare of the Ice Age to their doorstep.

A colossal pack of giant Dire Wolves (`Canis dirus`) was closing in through the frozen dark!
