# Chapter 011: The Secret Cliff Sanctuary & The Black Smoke Warning

The first rays of the sixth dawn breached the jagged crest of the limestone massif.
The dense, milky fog that had pooled across the canyon floor throughout the night coiled back under the encroaching heat of the rising sun.
Rime frost crusting the tips of late-autumn steppe grass melted into shimmering dew, dripping steadily onto the thawing permafrost.
The pungent musk of damp loam, resinous spruce sap, and woodsmoke from the central hearth drifted past the steel flank of Fortress Alpha.
Eighty meters south, downwind from the living quarters, Kuran and the hunters worked methodically on the seventy-five-kilogram carcass of the alpha dire wolf.
The SK5 utility knife in Kuran's scarred hand glided effortlessly through the dense hide, parting sinew and pelt like silk.
Thick slabs of rich, marbled venison and wolf fat were carved into strips, immediately coated with pure sea salt to cure for the impending deep winter.
Tara and the tribal women used smooth stones to scrape the leg tendons clean, setting them aside to dry into bowstrings and spear lashings.
The bloodied skull and offal were deposited deep into the sanitation pit, sealed securely inside heavy-duty black polyethylene garbage bags.
Eliminating the scent trail was Thame's unyielding sanitary doctrine, ensuring scavengers and predators would find no purchase near their camp.

Thame stood braced against the steel ladder of the Hino 500 Victor, taking in the crisp morning air.
In his hand was a thermos of warm boiled water and the fifth capsule of 500mg Amoxicillin.
"Take this medicine first, then drink every drop of the warm water," Thame instructed, handing the capsule to El-ya as she stepped out from the rainfly shelter.
The young huntress received the red-and-yellow capsule with trembling, reverent hands.
She tilted her head back and swallowed the synthetic remedy without hesitation, chasing it with the steaming mineral water from the thermos.
The vicious puncture wound on her right thigh, once swollen and weeping foul pus, was now dry and cleanly knit with pale pink scar tissue.
The septic fever and violent shivering that had nearly claimed her life had vanished completely, leaving her hawk-like hazel eyes sharp and clear.
Though she still favored her right leg, the hardened musculature of the paleolithic huntress was eager to move once more.

Thame's gaze drifted upward to the towering twenty-five-meter limestone cliff that loomed directly behind the truck's sealed cargo container.
With the morning air hovering at a brisk eight degrees Celsius, his eyes caught an anomaly fifteen meters above the truck's roofline.
Stunted mountain pine branches and thick curtains of ancient wild ivy clinging to a fissure in the rock were trembling softly.
The canyon floor below was dead calm, yet those hanging creepers were swaying as if caught in a steady exhaust draft.
A faint, translucent plume of warm vapor drifted continuously from behind the foliage into the crisp sunlight.
Thame's mechanical engineering training instantly recognized the physics at play.
This was no superficial crevice; it was an exhaust vent for a subterranean cavern housing an active thermal heat source.

Preparing for a vertical ascent, Thame climbed into the container's interior, scanning the dense stacks of cardboard parcels.
He pulled down a compact corrugated carton labeled `#TH-441092`, weighing 1.2 kilograms.
The waybill indicated shipment from a tactical gear and military field supply outfit.
Slitting the reinforced packing tape with his bushcraft blade, he revealed equipment that made his pulse quicken with quiet satisfaction.
Nestled in high-density foam were two **Rite in the Rain All-Weather Tactical Notebooks** bound in olive-drab synthetic covers.
The proprietary waterproof pages were impervious to rain, mud, blood, and freezing condensation.
Beside them lay an **Aircraft-Grade Aluminum Tactical Pressurized Pen** charged with nitrogen ink cartridges.
The pen was capable of writing at any angle, inverted, on wet surfaces, and down to minus twenty degrees Celsius.
The final item was a **High-Power 532nm Green Tactical Laser Pointer** equipped with an internal USB-rechargeable 18650 cell.
Its concentrated emerald beam could pierce atmospheric dust for several kilometers in a razor-sharp vector.
Thame tested the beam against the truck's steel wheel hub; the green focal point shone with blinding coherence.

Before taking a single step toward the ascent, Thame flipped open the virgin first page of the tactical notebook, pressing the pressurized ballpoint firmly to the synthetic sheet to draft his very first survival log—an indispensable psychological anchor to preserve his sanity and chronological footing in a world ten thousand years removed from home:

*[Thame's Survival Journal — Day 6 Post-Rift]*
*- Environment: Late Autumn, Younger Dryas Boundary, circa 10,000 BC. Morning temperature 8°C–12°C, polar drafts intensifying from the northern ridges.*
*- Bastion & Supplies: Hino 500 Victor ten-wheeler acting as permanent steel fortress. Cargo hold remains packed with unboxed parcels. Fuel and ammunition carefully preserved intact.*
*- Accomplishments to Date: Successfully cured El-ya's life-threatening septic wound using antibiotics, forged a bloodbound alliance with Clan Cliff Deer (Kuran, Moka, El-ya, and Tara) totaling thirty souls now encamped around the truck, established strict sanitary perimeter eliminating blood scent trails via black contractor bags, and decisively repelled eleven dire wolves using halogen high beams and air horn harmonics while neutralizing their alpha.*
*- Current Threats: Enemy scouts from the cannibalistic Beast Fang Clan sighted along ridges; copper-wire and bone war whistle recovered from wolf carcass.*
*- Today's Objective: Conduct vertical reconnaissance of thermal air fissure fifteen meters up the cliffside to secure a winter refuge for the entire settlement.*

Thame stared at the crisp black ink embedded in the green waterproof page with an immense sense of grounding relief. Seeing cold, calculated dates and tangible milestones written in his own hand proved that he was not merely surviving by chance—he was actively engineering civilization from the void.

"I have watched the rock breathe since I was a child... but our elders warned it is the nest of the sleeping cave spirits," El-ya murmured softly.
She looked at the laser pointer and the metal pen in Thame's hands with cautious awe, pointing up toward the suspended ivy curtain.
"If it breathes warm air, it belongs to no evil spirit. It is our fortress against the coming freeze," Thame replied firmly.
He clipped the tactical pen and waterproof notebook into his heavy parka, looping the laser pointer's lanyard around his wrist.

Thame knew that scaling a sheer cliff was dangerous for her newly healed quadriceps, but El-ya stubbornly insisted on guiding him to the sacred heights.
Taking the lead, Thame dug his steel-toed boots into the natural steps, turning to brace and hoist her up shelf by shelf.
The huntress gritted her teeth against the dull ache, driving her weight onto her uninjured left leg and relying on her powerful arms to pull herself upward.
Whenever a loose scree tested her balance, Thame’s firm, gloved grip steadied her, keeping her from reopening the delicate pink incision.
A quiet bond of absolute trust formed between them, forged on the wind-whipped rock face with every strained breath and offered hand.
Reaching the fifteen-meter shelf above the truck, Thame drew his fixed blade and parted the dense curtain of ancient creepers.
A waft of humid, twenty-four-degree air enveloped their faces, bringing immediate relief from the autumn chill.
Behind the greenery lay an oval cavern portal two meters wide, its limestone threshold dry and devoid of stale rot.
Only the subtle, clean scent of natural sulfur and geothermal steam rode the outgoing draft.

Passing through a narrow, three-meter stone bottleneck, the subterranean landscape expanded into breathtaking grandeur.
Before them lay a massive limestone rotunda stretching over thirty meters across and penetrating fifty meters into the mountain core.
The vaulted dome rose ten meters overhead, maintaining an astonishing ambient temperature of twenty-two to twenty-five degrees Celsius.
At the center of the cavern floor rested an expansive **Geothermal Emerald Hot Spring** nearly ten meters wide.
Its crystal-clear mineral waters reflected soft, rising plumes of steam that coiled gently toward the ceiling.
Thame knelt at the pool's calcified edge and dipped his bare fingers into the turquoise water.
The soothing warmth registered at approximately forty degrees Celsius, fed by subterranean aquifers linked to ancient volcanic faults.

"Sky-light... the roof is open to the sun!" El-ya gasped in wonder, her head tilted toward the vaulted ceiling.
Directly above the steaming thermal basin, a **Natural Limestone Chimney and Skylight** two meters in diameter bored through the cliff crest.
Midmorning sunlight poured down through the rock opening, striking the emerald pool in a shimmering pillar of liquid gold.
The physics of natural stack-effect ventilation were operating in absolute perfection.
Warm air and excess humidity were drawn continuously up through the chimney vent, pulling a steady stream of fresh air from the portal below.
The cavern remained dry, free of toxic carbon monoxide, and brightly illuminated with natural daylight.

Flanking the mineral pool were wide terraces of fine river sediment and sandy loam warmed by the water and direct solar exposure.
The soil was soft and temperate, entirely free of seasonal frost or permafrost.
"This is the ultimate subterranean nursery for winter crops..." Thame whispered, his mind racing through possibilities.
Deep in the eastern alcoves of the cavern, he spotted mounded deposits of desiccated **Ancient Bat Guano** over a meter thick.
It was a chemical goldmine—concentrated nitrogen and phosphorus fertilizer for agriculture, and raw potassium nitrate for future black powder synthesis.

El-ya stepped slowly into the shallow shelf of the emerald pool, letting the thermal water envelop her bruised, healing leg.
The geothermal mineral heat penetrated deep into stiffened ligaments, easing the chronic ache of the dire wolf bite in seconds.
The huntress closed her eyes, tears of profound relief cutting through the dust on her sun-darkened cheeks.
"The mountain spirit is not angry... it has given us a sanctuary of life to cleanse our wounds," she whispered in a trembling voice.

Thame unclipped the high-power green laser from his wrist and clicked the tailcap switch, casting the 532nm beam into the cavern's shadowed interior.
The piercing emerald lance swept across the smooth limestone walls, illuminating details that arrested Thame's breath in his throat.
Adorning the clean paleolithic stone was a sprawling gallery of **Upper Paleolithic Cave Art** rendered in brilliant red ochre and charcoal black.
Dozens of negative red hand stencils lined the lower walls alongside running silhouettes of woolly mammoths, Irish elks, and saber-toothed cats.
And at the apex of the chamber, directly beneath the skylight rim, was a massive circular petroglyph—a pitch-black disc surrounded by radiating solar flames.
A depiction of a total solar eclipse... or the primordial dimensional void that had cast him ten thousand years into the past!

Thame immediately drew his Rite in the Rain notebook, using the tactical pen to sketch the cavern's architectural layout, vents, and glyphs.
El-ya, stepping out from the emerald pool, watched in absolute fascination as dark ink flowed seamlessly from the metal stylus onto the synthetic paper.
She knelt beside him, gently touching the instantly dry ink. "Does this talisman... trap the shadow of human thoughts?"
Thame smiled faintly, seizing this pivotal moment to bridge the linguistic chasm dividing their worlds.
He pointed toward the tranquil geothermal water, enunciating clearly: "Water... Nam."
El-ya listened intently, her hazel eyes lighting up with swift comprehension before gesturing to the pool: "Aka..."
Thame noted it down immediately: [Aka = Water]. Next, he flicked his survival lighter, summoning a yellow flame: "Fire..."
"Tor..." El-ya responded without hesitation, her prehistoric intuition razor-sharp.
Thame pointed to his chest, then to hers: "Friend... Phuean..."
The huntress placed her calloused palm over her heart in a reverent gesture: "Sua..."
When Thame gestured toward the distant southern horizon with a cautious stance, El-ya's expression hardened into granite. "Orok..." she whispered gutturally.
"Orok means enemy... the Demon Fang clan," Thame murmured, recording vital tactical lexicon: [Sua = Friend, Orok = Enemy, Tah = Stop, Vah = Quick/Go].
El-ya earnestly practiced the strange syllables: "Frien'... Ra-wang... Nam..." Her accent was rough yet brimming with determination.
Little Tara, having scrambled up to the cave mouth, giggled in delight, echoing: "Phuean! Aka!"
The barrier of ten millennia began to crumble, bound together by synthetic paper, pressurised ink, and the bridge of shared speech.
Ascending the stepped limestone ledges beside the chimney, he squeezed through the upper shaft and emerged onto the clifftop observation ridge.
Standing high above Fortress Alpha, he commanded an unobstructed bird's-eye view across the entire prehistoric valley.
He drew his smartphone from his inner parka pocket, waking the display from its battery-saving slumber.
A single bar of 2G signal flickered steadily on the cliff crest; the battery rested securely at seventy-nine percent.
He snapped high-resolution photographs of the hand stencils, the eclipse petroglyph, and his hand-drawn survey map, uploading them to the forum thread.

The sleeping cyber war room erupted into a digital tempest, with thread view counters surging past fifty thousand within minutes.
`@PaleoArt_Sorbonne`: *"Good god! Those blown ochre hand stencils match the Chauvet Cave tradition, but the hematite pigmentation and megafaunal styling are distinctly Younger Dryas! That cavern is a ten-thousand-year archaeological time capsule!"*
`@EclipseHunter_NASA`: *"The blackened circle with radiating corona represents an ancient total solar eclipse! Orbital simulations show a total solar eclipse is due over that regional coordinate in the coming months!"*
Then, a crimson-highlighted urgent priority alert from tactical specialists `@CombatMedic_US` and `@Tactical_Recon` shattered the scientific revelry:
`"Thame! Zoom in on the wide-angle ridge photo you just uploaded! Look at the southern horizon twelve kilometers out!"`

Thame pulled his eyes from the screen, turning his gaze toward the jagged black peaks flanking the southern mountain pass.
His stomach dropped into an icy abyss as the cold autumn wind whipped across the exposed clifftop.
Rising from the distant ridge of obsidian monoliths were **Three Dense Pillars of Oily Black Smoke**, climbing relentlessly into the pale sky.
The thick, greasy columns, produced by burning resinous green spruce boughs and animal fat, were no natural forest fire.
They were tactical war beacons signaling the mobilization of the cannibalistic Demon Fang Clan!
The eerie war horn heard the night before had found its visual confirmation.
A ravenous tribal horde was gathering to sweep the valley, and the countdown to defend Fortress Alpha was ticking down by the hour!
