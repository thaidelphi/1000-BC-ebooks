# Chapter 1: The Black Fog and the Iron Bastion

The steel wipers dragged rhythmically across the windshield, emitting a steady, wet *screeech... screeech...* against the downpour. It contended with the deep, resonant drone of the 7.7-liter turbocharged inline-six diesel engine as it hauled twenty-five tons of gross weight up the winding asphalt of the Northern Mountain Pass.

Behind the wheel, Thame tightened his grip on the rim. At twenty-four, with an engineering degree still fresh in his drawer, he had stepped in to drive this afternoon haul for Uncle Det—his mother's elder brother and a lifelong trucker—who had been rushed into emergency appendectomy just hours earlier.

The assignment was straightforward: transport over 3,500 priority parcels across the crest to clear the regional logistics hub before nightfall.

When the ten-wheeler rolled out of the primary sorting depot at precisely 2:00 PM, an ordinary afternoon sun bathed the lowlands in bright, warm light.

Yet as the rig climbed into the silent folds of the pass, the horizon fractured into sheer madness.

A gargantuan wall of pitch-black fog, completely absent from every meteorological radar, avalanched down the sheer peaks like a dark landslide. The swirling mist was unnaturally dense, an opaque slurry of shadow that devoured the daylight in a matter of seconds.

From broad daylight at two-thirty, the sky plummeted into pitch-black oblivion. It was as dark as a moonless midnight—in the dead center of the afternoon.

*SHHHH-WOSHHH!*

Violent gusts howled through the gorge, pelting freezing rain and sleet against the cab like shotgun spray. Thame reached out and flicked the dual halogen headlamps and yellow fog lights to high. The twin beams barely punched ten meters into the impenetrable black murk before being swallowed whole.

The digital dashboard clock blinked: **14:47.**

Behind him, packed inside the 9.5-meter reinforced dry van container, rested over 3,500 courier parcels stacked tight to the roof. Corrugated cardboard boxes, waterproof poly mailers, and wooden crates—every piece tagged with barcodes and waybills. It was a blind, colossal stockpile of modern civil goods.

In that exact instant, the cabin temperature plunged below freezing!

*CRACKLE! SNAP!*

Arcs of violet-blue static electricity skittered wildly across the windshield frame and steel A-pillars. The sharp, acrid stench of ozone and scorched copper flared through the air vents. Gauges fluttered erratically; the air pressure needles spun into the red; and the CB radio let out a deafening screech before going dead.

"What the hell... Lightning?!" Thame cursed, white-knuckling the wheel.

Before his boot could depress the air brake pedal, the vista ahead twisted into a violent vortex. The asphalt beneath the tires ripped open into a bottomless dimensional tear.

*KABOOOOM!*

A catastrophic impact slammed into the rear quarter of the truck! An immense, violent force from something unseen in the pitch-black fog rammed the ten-wheeler, sending twenty-five tons of steel spinning in a violent three-hundred-and-sixty-degree pirouette. The steering wheel whipped out of his grip, and the front steer tires dropped into the vacuum.

Centrifugal force hurled his body violently against the cab pillar and the wheel.

Darkness slammed down.

---

A searing throb across his left temple and ribs dragged Thame back to consciousness.

He inhaled sharply. The sharp smell of motor oil, damp loam, and the unfamiliar icy scent of ancient pine invaded his nostrils. He was still strapped to the driver's seat, but the cab was pitched forward at an alarming thirty-degree angle. The laminated windshield was a spiderweb of cracks, though it hadn't shattered inward.

He tested his fingers, ankles, and neck. Nothing felt broken—just deep contusions on his shoulder and a split skin at his brow. He reached for his ruggedized smartphone.

Battery: 85%. Network status: *'No Service'*. GPS: spinning indefinitely without a coordinate fix. The digital lock screen was frozen dead at **14:47.**

The daylight of his home world was gone. Outside the glass stretched the gloomy, muted twilight of a dying day. The sky was an ominous vault of leaden fog, the sun already buried behind jagged peaks, threatening to disappear into complete night within minutes. A razor-sharp gale whistled through the door seals, and frost was already crystallizing across the dashboard plastics.

Gritting his teeth against the pain, Thame grabbed his tactical LED flashlight and a heavy one-meter steel wrecking bar wedged beside the driver's seat for protection.

He shoved the driver's door open and stepped down into the gathering dusk.

*FZZZZZZT!*

The sub-zero wind slashed across his cheeks like razor blades. Breath plumed from his mouth in thick white steam.

His heavy safety boots sank calf-deep into half-frozen mud. Raising the flashlight beam into the dusk, the scene before him forced him to freeze and reassess reality entirely.

No asphalt. No utility poles. No guardrails. No wreckage of other vehicles. No footprint of human civilization.

Under the faint, dying twilight, endless rolling tundra stretched to the horizon, carpeted with silvery frost. A few hundred meters out, a primeval taiga forest rose like a colossal timber wall, towering into the dim sky. Massive limestone cliffs loomed in the distant background. The last amber streak on the western horizon was fading fast, and the first solitary star blinked in the freezing sky.

Thame crouched down, sweeping the flashlight across the undercarriage. The damage was catastrophic.

The sheer kinetic force of twenty-five tons slamming through the rift had sheared both front 5160 leaf springs clean off their hangers. The tandem rear springs were buckled, the primary driveshaft twisted into a ruined corkscrew. The left front steer and rear dual tires were blown to shreds on their rims. The wheels sat buried to their axles in frozen sludge.

The truck was completely, irreversibly immobilized.

Worse, along the heavy rear underrun bumper and the right chassis rail, steel had been crushed inward in a deep groove. Scraped along the gash were flakes of foreign dark-blue and grey paint. Something heavy had undeniably rammed him before the spin... yet across the vast, bleak twilight, there was no trace of whatever hit him. Only silence.

In a prehistoric world ten thousand years before Christ, even if the suspension had survived, a twenty-five-ton highway rig could never drive here. There were no roads. No cart paths. Only glacial moraine, frozen bogs, and jagged rock. This truck was anchored as an **"Iron Bastion"** from the very first breath.

Yet by some grace, the heavy corrugated walls of the cargo box remained intact. The 250-liter aluminum diesel tank under the frame was undamaged—no leaks, holding 199.5 liters of fuel.

As the last daylight snuffed out and pitch-black night swallowed the tundra, Thame climbed onto the rear underrun bar, hauled on the locking rods, and swung open the dual steel container doors.

The familiar scent of cardboard, packing tape, and modern manufactured goods wafted out. The flashlight beam swept over a mountain of 3,500 sealed boxes crammed wall-to-wall in the twenty-four-square-meter hold. A blind cache of tens of thousands of survival resources.

*CRACK!*

A massive branch snapped in the timberline three hundred meters away.

Thame went rigid. He killed the flashlight instantly. Every sense spiked into red alert.

*THUD... THUD... THUD...*

A rhythmic, subterranean vibration traveled up through the frozen earth into his soles. Whatever was moving weighed at least a ton.

Pure survival instinct wrenched him into motion. Out here alone in the freezing black with nothing but a steel pry bar, he was prey.

He scrambled back into the driver's cab, slammed the heavy steel door, and threw the lock. Ducking low behind the seats, he choked his breathing down to silence in the total blackness.

The heavy strides halted right beside the driver's door.

*HUFFFF... HUFFFF...*

Hot, rank breath wheezed through the door seals. The musky, predatory reek of a massive carnivore mixed with old blood flooded the cramped cab, stinging his eyes. The beast was sniffing the strange steel monolith parked in its hunting territory.

*SCREEEEEEECH!*

Colossal claws raked across the outer door skin and A-pillar! The agonizing shriek of tearing metal rocked the entire ten-wheeler on its ruined springs. The door panel bowed inward with three deep gouges. The cracked windshield shuddered, raining fine glass dust into the cab.

*ROOOOOOOOAAAAAAARRRRRR!*

A thunderous, guttural roar detonated against the night, shaking the pines and echoing off the mountain wall. It was too deep, too primeval, too monstrous for any modern predator. It was the battle cry of an **Ice Age Cave Bear**—a prehistoric apex titan that had ruled this frozen earth ten millennia ago.

Thame's heart slammed against his ribs like a sledgehammer. Sub-zero cold bit through his jacket, shaking his limbs. He gripped the crowbar with slick, frozen palms. He knew with dreadful clarity: if this behemoth smashed through the cracked windshield, a steel rod would not save his life.

In that razor-edge between life and death, Uncle Det's gravelly voice echoed through his memory:
*'Thame... if anything goes south out on a desolate road, I’ve got a locked box under the sleeper bunk. Don’t you ever forget it!'*

Thame dropped to his knees in the pitch dark. Feeling along the worn velvet mattress of the sleeper cab, he found the latch and jammed the wedge of the crowbar into the seam, popping the hidden lock with a muffled snap.

Inside the recessed steel box, his hands found four lifelines:

1. **Sub-Zero Arctic Sleeping Bag (-15°C):** Thick, lofty goose down—salvation from hypothermia on night one.
2. **10-Inch High-Carbon Steel Bowie Knife:** Full-tang, razor-sharp, balanced perfectly in hand.
3. **1.5-Liter Stainless Vacuum Flask:** Still holding warm water from earlier that morning.
4. **Heavy Black Steel Lockbox:** Prying the latch open made his breath catch... resting inside was a **Smith & Wesson .38 Special 3-Inch Revolver**, blued steel gleaming in the dark, accompanied by a green cardboard ammunition box containing semi-wadcutter lead rounds. Exactly **43 cartridges** in total (5 loaded in the cylinder, 38 in the paper tray).

Thame checked the cylinder by touch with practiced familiarity. Slipping into the arctic sleeping bag, he braced his back against the steel bulkhead, leveled the .38 revolver through the dark directly at the door, and staged his finger against the trigger. If that beast breached the steel, he would fire point-blank.

For thirty agonizing minutes, the titan circled and tested the iron walls before the heavy thuds finally faded into the vast tundra night. Silence reclaimed the wilderness.

Thame slowly exhaled, willing his racing pulse back to steady calm. His gaze stayed fixed on the deep claw marks pressed into the door steel, his right hand firmly wrapped around the grip of the .38 beneath the down bag.

The reality was absolute.
This was no missing territory on a modern map.
This was the **Late Glacial Maximum of 10,000 BC**—a brutal stone-age world where mankind survived beneath the shadows of prehistoric monsters. And this crippled ten-wheel fortress, packed with thousands of blind parcels, was the solitary anchor that would decide whether he would live or perish in the wild dawn of antiquity.
