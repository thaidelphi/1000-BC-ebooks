# Chapter 1: The Black Fog and the Iron Bastion

The steel wipers dragged rhythmically across the windshield, emitting a steady, wet *screeech... screeech...* against the downpour. It contended with the deep, resonant drone of the 7.7-liter turbocharged inline-six diesel engine as it hauled twenty-five tons of gross weight up the winding asphalt of the Northern Mountain Pass.

Behind the wheel, Thame tightened his grip on the rim. At twenty-four, with an engineering degree fresh in hand, he held a Type 2 commercial transport license earned during his university years.

He was no novice behind the wheel of a heavy rig; he had substituted for Uncle Det, his maternal uncle and a veteran highway hauler, countless times whenever his schedule allowed. He was thoroughly familiar with the transmission splits, gear timing, and every mechanical quirk of this ten-wheeler.

When Uncle Det was stricken by sudden acute appendicitis and rushed into emergency surgery earlier that morning, Thame was the only driver trusted to seamlessly take over this urgent afternoon run.

The assignment was straightforward: transport over 3,500 priority courier parcels across the rugged northern mountain ridge to clear the regional logistics hub before nightfall.

When the ten-wheeler rolled out of the primary sorting depot at precisely 2:00 PM, an ordinary afternoon sun bathed the lowlands in bright, warm light.

The highway wound steadily upward into the steep mountain ridges, bordered on both sides by dense emerald foliage and the cool, humid mist of the highland jungle.

Yet as the rig climbed deeper into the silent folds of the pass, the atmosphere fractured into sheer madness.

A gargantuan wall of pitch-black fog, completely absent from every meteorological radar, avalanched down the sheer limestone cliffs like a dark landslide.

The swirling mist was unnaturally dense, an opaque slurry of shadow that devoured the daylight in a matter of seconds.

From broad daylight at two-thirty, the sky plummeted into pitch-black oblivion with terrifying speed... It was as dark as a moonless midnight—in the dead center of the afternoon.

*SHHHH-WOSHHH!*

Violent gusts howled through the mountain pass, pelting torrential rain and blinding mist against the cab like shotgun spray.

Thame reached out and flicked the dual halogen headlamps and auxiliary fog lights to high. The twin beams barely punched ten meters into the impenetrable black murk before being swallowed whole.

The digital dashboard clock blinked: **14:47.**

Behind him, packed inside the 9.5-meter reinforced dry van container, rested over 3,500 courier parcels stacked tight to the roof. Corrugated cardboard boxes, waterproof poly mailers, and wooden crates—every piece tagged with barcodes and waybills. It was a blind, colossal stockpile of modern civil goods.

In that exact instant, the air pressure and cabin temperature plunged in an unnatural shudder. A biting, humid chill crept in through the air vents.

*CRACKLE! SNAP!*

Arcs of violet-blue static electricity skittered wildly across the windshield frame and steel A-pillars. The sharp, acrid stench of ozone and scorched copper flared through the cab.

Gauges fluttered erratically; the air brake pressure needles spun into the red; and the CB radio let out a deafening screech before going dead.

"What the hell... Lightning?!" Thame cursed through gritted teeth, white-knuckling the wheel.

Before his boot could depress the air brake pedal, visibility collapsed entirely into swirling blinding white. Air pressure slammed against the windshield with disorienting violence.

*KABOOOOM!*

A catastrophic impact slammed into the rear quarter of the truck!

An immense, violent force from something unseen in the pitch-black fog rammed the ten-wheeler, sending twenty-five tons of steel spinning in a violent three-hundred-and-sixty-degree pirouette.

The steering wheel whipped violently out of his grip. The runaway rig tore through the gravel shoulder, pitching sideways over the embankment and sliding down the sheer mountain slope into the black void below!

The undercarriage scraped violently against exposed bedrock with a deafening roar. Centrifugal force hurled his body against the door pillar and the steering wheel with crushing force!

Darkness slammed down in an instant.

---

A searing throb across his left temple and ribs dragged Thame back to consciousness.

He inhaled sharply. The sharp smell of motor oil, damp loam, and the unfamiliar cool scent of ancient highland pines invaded his nostrils.

He was still strapped to the driver's seat, but the cab was pitched forward at an alarming thirty-degree angle. The laminated windshield was a spiderweb of cracks, though it hadn't shattered inward.

He tested his fingers, ankles, and neck. Nothing felt broken—just deep contusions on his shoulder and a split skin at his brow.

He reached for his ruggedized smartphone to check the display.

Battery: 85%. Network status: *'No Service'*. GPS: spinning indefinitely without a coordinate fix.

The clock on his phone screen was running normally, displaying **17:52**—revealing that he had been knocked out cold for nearly three full hours after the crash at 14:47! (Meanwhile, the truck's dashboard clock remained dead and frozen at 14:47, killed the moment the impact severed electrical power.)

The daylight of his home world was gone. Outside the glass stretched the gloomy, muted twilight of a dying day.

The sky was an ominous vault of thick fog rolling over the valley, the sun already buried behind jagged limestone peaks, threatening to disappear into complete night within minutes.

A cool, damp mountain gale whistled through the door seals, mist beading along the dashboard plastics.

Gritting his teeth against the pain, Thame grabbed his tactical LED flashlight and a heavy one-meter steel wrecking bar wedged beside the driver's seat for protection.

He shoved the driver's door open and stepped down into the gathering dusk.

*FZZZZZZT!*

A cool gale of the ancient forest rushed against his face. His breath puffed out in faint wisps of white vapor, carrying the crisp chill of the mountain valley at dusk.

His steel-toed boots sank deep into soggy loam choked with decaying leaves, submerging his ankles.

Sweeping the flashlight beam through the murky dimness, the panorama froze him in his tracks to reassess his reality:

No asphalt highway. No utility poles. No debris of any other vehicle, nor any trace of human civilization.

In the fading gloom of twilight, surrounding him was a vast open expanse of grassland punctuated by jagged limestone boulders at the floor of the valley.

Further out lay a towering forest of ancient two-needle pines (*Pinus kesiya*) and primeval deciduous hardwoods rising straight into the sky. Massive limestone peaks formed a natural fortress, encircling the valley on all sides.

A dense sea of white mist was already flooding the ravines. The last amber glow on the horizon faded, and the first solitary star flickered above as the damp chill of the mountain forest rolled in.

Thame crouched down, directing the beam beneath the chassis. The extent of mechanical destruction was staggering:

The massive kinetic energy of twenty-five tons skidding down the precipitous mountain slope had shattered both front leaf spring packs and snapped the rear tandem packs clean off their hangers. The central driveshaft was twisted like a pretzel. The front left and rear dual tires were shredded to the rims, bogged deep in stubborn, sticky clay. The vehicle was completely and irreversibly immobilized!

Furthermore, on the heavy rear bumper and right chassis corner, a deep, mangled gash was crushed inward, proving unequivocally that something with immense mass had rammed them hard before the skid... Yet shining the beam up the steep crags or into the gloom revealed nothing. No rocks, no vehicles—only an eerie, suffocating silence.

In a remote ravine this rugged and deep, even if the suspension had survived, a twenty-five-ton ten-wheeler had zero hope of climbing back to any road. There were no access trails, no graded paths—only limestone crags, broken terrain, and mud bogs.

The stranded rig was permanently pinned as an **"immobile iron fortress."**

Fortunately, the heavy steel container box remained intact. The aluminum diesel fuel tank beneath the belly was solidly mounted, showing no cracks or leaks. The full tank of diesel pumped before departure was nearly untouched.

As the last light died and night took full possession of the wilderness, Thame climbed around to the rear, unlatched the heavy container handles, and swung the double doors open to inspect the cargo in his charge.

The scent of dry cardboard, packing tape, and modern merchandise drifted out. His flashlight illuminated a mountain of over 3,500 courier parcels stacked tight. Though several stacks had tilted from the impact, the vast majority were undamaged.

Thame exhaled a long breath of relief. At least his customers' parcels were safe and not smashed beyond repair.

He had no intention of touching any of these goods. Every package belonged to someone who had paid for it, and until every other avenue was exhausted, he would never violate that trust. He firmly pushed the doors shut and fastened the heavy latches.

*CRACK!*

The sharp snap of a heavy tree bough echoed from the pine woods some three hundred meters away.

Thame froze, instantly killing the flashlight. Every sense sharpened to a razor's edge.

*THUD... THUD... THUD...*

A dull tremor rippled through the damp loam beneath his boots. The weight of the creature behind those steps easily exceeded a ton.

Primal fear surged through his veins. He had no idea what was moving out there, but alone in an ancient, pitch-black forest with only a steel pry bar, he had zero chance of survival!

He lunged back toward the cab, hauled himself up into the cockpit, slammed the heavy door, and clicked the deadbolt shut. Sinking low behind the driver's seat, he muted his breathing to a whisper in the pitch darkness.

The heavy footfalls ceased right beside the driver's door...

*FSSSHHH... FSSSHHH...*

Hot, heavy respiration wheezed through the rubber weatherstripping. The pungent, rank stench of an apex predator mingled with dried blood filled the air, so thick it stung his nostrils. It was pacing, sniffing at this strange metal monolith that had crashed into its hunting grounds.

*SKKKRRRREEECH!*

Massive claws raked violently across the outer steel door panel and cab pillar! The shriek of tortured sheet metal was ear-splitting, rocking the twenty-five-ton rig on its damaged axles.

The heavy steel plate buckled inward, scarred with three jagged gouges. The cracked windshield shuddered, sending tiny shards of tempered glass raining onto the dash.

*ROOOOAAARRRR!*

A deafening, earth-shaking roar tore through the ancient forest, echoing violently across the limestone cliffs. The sound was guttural, primeval, and possessed power far beyond any living carnivore he had ever known.

Thame had never heard of any beast in this country growing to such monstrous proportions. But the sheer weight and force behind the attack made one thing crystal clear: an apex titan stood outside!

His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. The damp chill of the mountain night penetrated deep into his bones, making his frame shiver. His hands, gripping the cold steel bar, were slick with icy sweat. He knew that if this beast decided to ram through the fractured windshield, a pry bar was nothing more than a toothpick!

In that razor-thin margin between life and death, Thame refused to surrender to panic. Having driven this rig for Uncle Det many times, he knew every emergency supply and where it was stowed like the back of his hand.

Reaching forward, Thame grabbed Uncle Det's **stainless vacuum flask (1.5 liters)** sitting in the console cup holder. The herbal tea Uncle Det had brewed that morning still held residual warmth, offering vital heat against the encroaching chill. He then retreated into the velvet-lined sleeper berth behind the seats.

In the dark, his hands deftly unlatched the concealed steel storage compartment beneath the sleeper mattress—a familiar stash for long-haul security:

1. **Sub-zero Down Sleeping Bag:** Thick, plush, and exceptionally insulating, capable of keeping him warm against the highland chill on night one.
2. **10-inch Carbon Steel Bowie Knife:** Razor-sharp with substantial chopping weight.
3. **Black Steel Safety Box:** Inside lay a blued **Smith & Wesson .38 Special Revolver (3-inch barrel)** alongside a green cardboard box holding **43 rounds** of semi-wadcutter lead ammunition (5 loaded in the cylinder, 38 resting in the carton tray).

Thame was no professional marksman. He was an engineering graduate who had merely been shown the basic cylinder release and loading procedures by Uncle Det for highway emergencies. With numb, trembling fingers, he held the heavy blued steel.

His cold thumb located the cylinder release latch, swinging the cylinder outward. In the pitch darkness, he traced the rims of all five chambers with his thumb, confirming they were loaded before clicking the cylinder back into frame with a decisive *snick!*

Crawling into the down sleeping bag, he backed against the steel cab pillar. With both hands still trembling from cold and adrenaline, he leveled the .38 revolver through the gloom straight at the door, heart racing, prepared to pull the trigger in desperate self-defense if the steel gave way.

After circling and testing the perimeter of the iron fortress for nearly half an hour, the heavy footfalls finally receded into the dark forest, returning silence to the primeval valley.

Thame exhaled a shuddering breath, willing his pulse to settle. Through the darkness, his eyes lingered on the three deep claw marks grooved into the steel door.

Once certain the predator had moved off, he gently placed the loaded .38 revolver by his side on the sleeper mattress within instant reach, then bundled deep inside the sleeping bag to fend off the mountain cold.

He still clung to the belief that he had simply suffered a freak rollover off a remote northern highway pass. But the primeval karst wilderness, the enveloping fog, and the roar of a monstrous beast that had just retreated whispered an ominous truth...

Whatever he was facing, it was hundreds of times more dangerous than an ordinary highway accident!

In this freezing, pitch-black wilderness, this ten-wheeled iron fortress and the steel revolver beside him were all that stood between him and the dark dawn to come.
