# Chapter 10: The Spotlight Hunt

An unnatural silence descended upon Horseshoe Gorge like a suffocating shroud of death.

The mournful, reverberating howls that had echoed off the surrounding limestone cliffs ceased entirely.

Yet this sudden dead silence carried a dread far more terrifying than any primeval beast's battle cry.

A freezing northern wind whistled through the jagged rock clefts, wailing like the lamentations of the dead.

The brittle frost coating the windshield of the ten-wheeler thickened into an opaque veneer of ice.

Midnight temperatures plunged toward 7°C, each human breath pluming into thick, pure white clouds against the cold.

Beneath their boots, the gravel soil hardened brittle; dry pine needles snapped with distinct cracks underfoot.

Thirty meters out in the pitch-black shadows, massive silhouettes slowly shifted into an encircling formation.

Emerald and amber eyes glinted in the dying firelight, advancing toward Fortress Alpha with military discipline.

They were a pack of dire wolves (*Canis dirus*), full-grown Pleistocene apex carnivores averaging eighty kilograms each.

Thick ruffs of coarse, dark gray fur guarded muscular shoulders built for explosive acceleration.

Their massive lower jaws bristled with conical carnassial teeth capable of crushing a giant deer's femur to dust in a single bite.

Inside the perimeter of Fortress Alpha, the suffocating atmosphere clamped down on every trembling heart.

Elderly women and children huddled beneath the nylon flysheet beside the container, weeping softly in sheer panic.

Little Tara clung tightly to her mother's chest, wide eyes tracking the glowing predator eyes advancing in the dark.

Ten able-bodied hunters of the Mountain Deer Clan stood in a defensive rank, calloused hands rattling stone spear shafts against one another.

Kuran barked a raspy rallying cry, while Moga knelt behind them chanting desperate prayers to ancestral spirits.

A young hunter hurled a burning pine torch forward into the darkness to intimidate the pack.

Yet the feeble orange flame failed to faze the giant predators; the wolves parted around the torch with chilling composure.

They understood well that humans were virtually blind in the moonless dark, and small campfires could never protect the wide flank of the vehicle.

The lead two wolves began pacing toward the massive truck tires, hot breath puffing in rhythmic bursts against the biting cold.

"Fall back to the tires! Don't take their charge in the open! Stone spears can't stop that mass!" Thame shouted, backing his words with sharp gestures.

He knew full well that in close-quarters melee, a dire wolf's bone-crushing jaws would sever prehistoric limbs in seconds.

In that critical heartbeat, Thame bolted back inside the dark container, sweeping his LED beam across the stacked cardboard cartons.

His light locked onto a compact parcel labeled `#TH-883109`, gross weight 1.4 kilograms.

The thermal shipping manifest was unmistakable: *Tactical Field Lighting & Mil-Spec Rigging Cord*.

Flicking open his rescue multi-tool knife, Thame sliced through the packing tape and yanked back the flaps.

Inside lay two high-output zoomable LED tactical headlamps, housed in matte-black aircraft-grade aluminum.

Powered by Cree XHP50 chips delivering up to 1,500 lumens, paired with fully charged 18650 lithium-ion cells.

Beneath them lay a 50-meter coil of olive-drab Mil-Spec 550 paracord, its seven nylon inner strands boasting tremendous tensile strength.

Thame ran his thumb over the tightly woven paracord with immense relief—a priceless asset for rigging perimeter snares and tripwires tomorrow.

He snatched the first headlamp, strapped the heavy elastic band around his brow, and clicked the power switch.

*Flash!*

A blinding, pure white beam erupted like a lightning spear, cutting through mist and shadow over a hundred meters out.

Leaping down from the cargo deck, Thame aimed the 1,500-lumen spotlight straight down the gorge, illuminating eleven stalking dire wolves.

The beasts recoiled in shock, primeval retinas conditioned to perpetual dark assaulted by artificial glare.

Thame tossed the second headlamp to El-ya, who was leaning against the front bumper with her spear.

The huntress strapped it over her forehead by instinct, powering on the second daylight beam to form a crossfire of illumination.

Yet the massive alpha male—a scarred silver-gray beast nearly the size of a pony—growled deep in his chest.

Lowering his massive shoulders and pinning back his ears, the alpha prepared to lead a full-throttle charge into the camp.

"If headlamps won't break them... let's see how they handle the real iron beast!" Thame snarled, his decision made.

He scrambled into the Hino 500 Victor's cab and slammed the heavy door, shutting out the feral snarls.

Thrusting the ignition key into the slot, he clicked it to ON, engaging the diesel glow plugs.

Analog dashboard dials flared amber as the twin 24V batteries delivered full voltage.

Thame flipped the master switch for the dual high-beam halogen headlights and fog lamps.

Then, he torqued the ignition key all the way to START!

*Chug... chug... ROAAARRRRRR!*

The 380-horsepower, 6-cylinder turbocharged intercooled diesel engine detonated into life with a ferocious, earth-shaking rumble.

The ground trembled beneath the pistons' rhythm; the thunderous mechanical roar tore the ten-thousand-year silence to shreds.

Plumes of diesel exhaust pulsed from the undercarriage pipe, swirling into the freezing night air.

Twin halogen floodlights unleashed an intense curtain of brilliant white light over two hundred meters down the gorge.

Towering limestone cliffs cast sharp, geometric shadows across the canyon—the first time in history that night was banished like high noon.

Caught dead-center in the blazing beam, the dire wolves suffered instant flashblindness.

They shook their massive heads in agony, yelping and snapping blindly at the empty air.

Before they could recover, Thame slammed his palm down hard onto the dual air horn valve on the steering wheel!

*HOOOOONK—BLAAAST!*

A 130-decibel pneumatic sonic blast thundered down the limestone corridor, rebounding off the forty-meter cliffs in dozens of concussive echoes.

The Mountain Deer Clan collapsed to their knees, clapping hands over their ears in terror before the "sacred roar of the Iron Sky Titan."

The dire wolf pack shattered in absolute panic, slamming into boulders and each other as they scrambled to escape the sound.

Yet the giant alpha male stood his ground, shaking his scarred head and snarling with rabid defiance, stepping forward to bite the spinning tires.

In that razor-thin moment, El-ya stood bathed in the truck's headlights, setting her spear against the wheel and drawing her hunting bow to full draw.

In the brilliant illumination, the huntress had never seen her target with such immaculate, effortless clarity.

Her dark eyes were cold as glacial ice; the tactical headlamp painted the beast's jugular dead-center.

*Twang—THWACK!*

The razor-sharp flint arrowhead ripped through the cold air, driving clean through the alpha's windpipe and carotid artery up to the fletching.

The giant carnivore spasmed violently, dark crimson spraying across its silver-gray fur, before crashing heavily onto the frosted gravel.

Seeing their commander fall, the surviving wolves broke into a frenzied rout, vanishing down into the primeval pine forest.

Thame turned the key and killed the diesel engine after just five minutes to preserve precious fuel.

The short run consumed exactly 1.0 liter of diesel (leaving 198.5 liters remaining) and lowered brake air pressure to 6.6 bar.

Peace settled once more over Horseshoe Gorge, the crisp air tainted with the faint, sharp tang of diesel exhaust.

Thirty members of the Mountain Deer Clan erupted into ecstatic cheers, bowing reverently toward the truck and Thame in profound worship.

Little Tara bounded forward, hugging Thame's leg, while Kuran touched the young engineer's palm to swear eternal loyalty.

Thame walked forward with El-ya to examine the massive carcass of the fallen alpha lying in a pool of freezing blood.

Using his SK5 utility blade, he parted the thick, bloody fur around the beast's throat.

But as the beam of his headlamp illuminated the wolf's neck, the smile vanished from Thame's face.

Around the beast's muscular throat was no natural fur collar.

It was a thick, braided leather collar tightly stitched with drawn copper wire.

The presence of processed metal wire in 10,000 BC was an impossible anomaly that screamed of modern knowledge.

And suspended at the center of the collar was a human-femur war whistle carved with blood-red skull glyphs.

These predators had not come to hunt by wild instinct... they were "war hounds" bred and commanded by the Demon Fang Clan!

And high upon the jagged cliffs above the canyon, a faint, chilling blast from an ancient ivory war horn echoed into the night... signaling that their masters were watching from the shadows!
