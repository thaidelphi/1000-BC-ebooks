# Chapter 26: The Horseshoe Valley Coalition & Post-War Integration

*Clang! Thud! Scrape!*

The sharp clatter of over eighty flint spears, bone daggers, and fire-hardened wooden clubs studded with wolf fangs crashing onto the snow before the fortress rang out continuously, echoing off the towering limestone cliffs.

Over eighty surviving warriors of the Demon Fang Clan knelt flat upon the freezing snow until their knees went numb. Shoulders that had once bristled with savage ferocity now trembled from biting cold and sheer terror. Every pair of blood-rimmed eyes remained fixed on the crest of the earth-filled ramparts and the colossal ten-wheeled truck.

They awaited the fate they knew so intimately in the merciless Stone Age—mass slaughter, decapitated heads impaled on stakes, or being flayed alive, exactly as their clan had done to others in victory.

The gaunt faces of these barbarian fighters were hollow-cheeked, their lips cracked and caked with dried blood from prolonged starvation. The fingers and toes of many had already turned blackish-purple from frostbite, which had gnawed away at their flesh throughout their grueling march across the mountain ranges.

Kuran stood vigilant atop the edge of the rammed-earth wall, his thick hand gripping the shaft of his leaf-spring steel spear so tightly that veins bulged along his forearm. The eyes of the Mountain Deer champion burned with vengeance accumulated over years of bloodshed.

Vivid memories of the Demon Fangs raiding their ancestral grounds, butchering kin, and torching shelters surged through his mind. Kuran turned to lock eyes with Thame, pointing his thumb downward toward the crimson-stained snow and drawing his finger slowly across his throat, asking for the command to execute every last enemy and avenge the fallen.

Thame descended from the rear ladder of the container truck with calm, measured steps. Walking steadily across the snowy courtyard, he placed a gentle hand on Kuran's broad shoulder before shaking his head in firm refusal:

"No, Kuran... The era of tribal slaughter ends today."

The young man understood deeply that as the Ice Age destabilized into the brutal Younger Dryas cooling event, humanity's true enemy was not other humans, but nature's unrelenting cruelty. Slaying eighty able-bodied young men in cold blood was an act of reckless biological waste.

What Fortress Alpha needed was not a mound of rotting corpses, but muscle and labor to erect a settlement, clear land, dig irrigation channels, and stockpile firewood so that the human species could survive together.

Suddenly, hurried footsteps crunching through the snow sounded from the southern pass. Borg and eight pine nomads emerged, escorting a group of twelve women and children liberated from the rear lines of the Demon Fang warband.

"Ata!... Meya!" Borg let out a deep, resounding roar, dropping his recurve bow onto the snow as he sprinted forward to embrace a small boy and a young woman from his clan who had been captured into slavery two weeks prior.

Tears of profound relief streamed down the weathered, frostbitten cheeks of the old hunter. The small boy buried his face against his father's chest, sobbing uncontrollably in relief after being miraculously rescued from a living nightmare.

The liberated women and children were clad only in tattered, ragged scraps of old reindeer hide, their bodies emaciated to the bone. They stood shivering in the biting sub-zero wind howling through the gorge.

Thame stepped toward Borg, offering the universal hunter sign of peace by touching two fingers to his own forehead, then gesturing openly toward the liberated captives:

"Pine nomads... Take your kin and your women back into your arms. This camp holds no place for innocent slaves."

Elya reinforced the words with fluent hunter hand signs. Borg stared at Thame's composed face in stunned disbelief. The veteran hunter glanced at the children, unharmed and whole, before falling to both knees upon the snow, pressing his forehead directly against the frozen earth before Thame's boots.

The old hunter reached out, offering a broken pine branch as a sacred token of absolute surrender and lifelong fealty. The blood pact of the pine nomads was now irrevocably bound to the Iron Bastion.

Tara and a group of Mountain Deer women quickly brought out thick wolf-skin mantles, wrapping them around the shivering children and mothers. Meanwhile, Elya knelt beside a trembling little girl who was shivering uncontrollably.

Kneeling in the snow, Elya produced a tender strip of smoked venison and a hollow bamboo cylinder filled with warm water, offering them with a warm, comforting smile.

The child stared at Elya's gentle face with brief suspicion before grasping the meat and tearing into it ravenously. The warm water thawed the chill in her throat, and the faint bloom of an innocent smile surfaced on her face, melting the grim tension of war in an instant.

Meanwhile, near the camp's main gate, Dr. Nicha was engrossed in performing emergency triage on Grak, the fallen warlord.

The two-meter colossus of the southern valley lay sprawled across a heavy bamboo stretcher, his breathing shallow and strained from sheer exhaustion. The second-degree blistering burn across his chest, inflicted by the white-hot magnesium flare, was being washed gently with sterile boiled saline by Dr. Nicha. The sharp, acrid stench of singed animal fur and scorched flesh hung heavily in the freezing air.

Using sterile surgical forceps, the physician patiently debrided charred bear-pelt fragments that had melted into the blistered epidermis. Though Grak clenched his jaw so violently that corded muscles bulged along his neck in agony, he did not utter a single defiant sound.

"Don't tense up... This salve will extinguish the fire in your wound," Nicha spoke in a soothing yet authoritative tone, exuding the unshakable confidence of a trauma surgeon.

Using a wooden spatula, she scooped a generous layer of amber-colored herbal Mebo burn ointment, coating the charred skin with a thick, protective balm. The cooling moisture and therapeutic properties of the sesame and beta-sitosterol immediately extinguished the searing flame eating through Grak's chest muscles.

The warlord, who had previously groaned in agony, slowly opened his eyes. His slate-gray eyes, once ravenous for slaughter, were now filled with reverence and awe as he watched this woman in a pristine white coat wrapping soft, snow-white bandages around his torso without demanding his life in return.

Grak glanced up at Thame, who had walked over to stand beside the stretcher. The truck driver carried neither the flare gun nor any weapon, displaying only a steady, resolute gaze.

The barbarian commander swallowed his dry throat, struggling with all his might to hoist his muscular, hundred-kilogram frame into a sitting posture. He placed the shattered remains of his stone maul at Thame's boots, then bowed his forehead flat against the snow, rumbling a hoarse, guttural chant from deep within his chest:

"Tor... Suwa... Aca!"

He yielded unconditionally to the power of the divine flame, pledging his life to the master of the iron fortress—a ruler who possessed not only the power to destroy, but the mercy to restore life.

"If you understand friendship... we will survive this winter together," Thame said calmly, giving Grak's massive shoulder a reassuring pat.

Yet the greatest logistical crisis following the end of hostilities was feeding more than one hundred and twenty souls in an instant!

If eighty captive warriors were left to starve, hunger would inevitably reignite their feral instincts and spark bloody rebellion. But if nourishment and genuine care were used to bind their hearts, these men would become a bulwark of loyalty stronger than any stone rampart.

Thame instructed Kuran and Moga to set up a massive field kitchen in the central courtyard, mounting two halved steel drums as oversized cauldrons over blazing pine hearths reinforced with limestone blocks.

Pure stream water was channeled through bamboo flumes to fill both cauldrons to the brim. Stockpiled Irish elk bones, wild starchy tubers, and dried venison strips were dropped in, simmering until the broth rolled in a vigorous boil, sending mouthwatering steam swirling across the valley.

Moga contributed dried medicinal forest roots to ward off scurvy and aromatic wild mushrooms, while Kuran used an axe to cleave massive marrow bones so that rich, nutrient-dense marrow dissolved into the broth.

Then, Thame retrieved half a concentrated beef bouillon cube, crumbling it into the boiling liquid, followed by two generous handfuls of pure, sparkling white sea salt—precisely fifty grams—scattered into the swirling broth.

The pristine sea salt, devoid of the bitter, alkaline astringency of prehistoric dirt licks, instantly fused with animal lipids and savory beef essence, transforming simple bone water into an elixir packed with the vital electrolytes necessary for winter survival.

Carved wooden bowls and split bamboo cups brimming with piping-hot soup were distributed to every Demon Fang warrior huddled shivering around the fire pits.

The moment the scalding broth touched their tongues... an explosion of warmth and rich, profound savoriness unlike anything they had ever tasted flooded their mouths.

Pure sodium and vital minerals coursed through their bloodstreams, instantly restoring energy depleted by weeks of brutal campaigning and freezing cold. Their eyes dilated in sheer shock before tears of overwhelmed gratitude cascaded down their soot-streaked cheeks.

Many knelt to lick their wooden bowls until not a single drop remained. Others touched their foreheads to their empty bowls upon the snow, bowing as if worshiping a holy relic.

In a Stone Age world where men routinely slaughtered one another over putrid scraps of meat, receiving a steaming bowl of pure salted meat broth from their conqueror was a miraculous act of grace that transformed blood enemies into sworn brothers in a single meal.

Yet warmth from broth alone could not fend off the impending blizzard. Fortress Alpha's existing quarters were built to house only forty people. With the population suddenly surging beyond a hundred, erecting a massive communal log shelter became the most pressing survival emergency.

Thame retreated into the dry container truck, moving past the Green and Blue Zones toward the hardware shelving racks.

His eyes locked onto a heavy, reinforced corrugated carton bearing Waybill `#TH-392811`, weighing 8.5 kilograms and addressed to a construction hardware partnership.

Drawing his 1095 high-carbon bushcraft knife, the young man sliced through the heavy packing tape to reveal its contents:

Inside lay 4 drop-forged high-carbon steel felling axe heads, finished in matte black with razor-sharp ground bits, complete with wooden and steel cross-wedges.

A heavy burlap sack packed with 5 kilograms of rust-resistant hot-dipped galvanized timber nails in 3-inch and 4-inch lengths—approximately 750 nails in total.

And 2 heavy-duty claw hammers featuring fiberglass shafts wrapped in shock-absorbing rubber grips.

"This is exactly what we need to build our city!" Thame declared with a hopeful grin.

In the Stone Age, joining timber demanded hours of painstaking carving with flint chisels and binding with fragile animal sinew or bark cordage. Having galvanized nails and forged steel axe heads represented an evolutionary leap of ten thousand years in structural engineering.

Thame fitted one of the forged axe heads onto a cured oak handle, driving the steel wedges firmly into the eye. Stepping out into the camp courtyard, he rallied Kuran's carpenters and Grak's able-bodied warriors.

The young man demonstrated by burying the steel bit into a dry pine trunk. With just three clean, effortless swings, the dense wood was sheared through cleanly—a task that would have shattered flint adzes and battered human hands for hours.

Next, he laid two heavy logs across one another, raised the fiberglass claw hammer, and drove a 4-inch galvanized nail straight through the timber with pinpoint precision:

*Tham! Tham! Tham!*

With three rhythmic strikes, the nail head sank flush, locking both timbers together with immovable rigidity.

Kuran, Grak, and the surrounding warriors gaped in absolute wonder. Grak reached out, testing the joint with his immense brute strength, but the joined timber refused to budge even a fraction of an inch.

The barbarian warlord pointed a finger toward the sky, then thudded his own chest in humble reverence toward the miraculous intellect of the modern world.

Thame immediately organized the workforce into four strategic brigades to brace for the coming deep freeze:

Brigade One, the 'Timber Felling & Logging Unit,' saw Grak and ten giant warriors armed with the new steel axes, utilizing their monstrous physical power to fell straight pine trees in the inner valley for house pillars.

The raw physical power of Stone Age humans combined with razor-sharp modern steel caused towering pines to crash down one after another at an unbelievable pace.

Brigade Two, the 'Shelter Construction Unit,' led by Kuran and Mountain Deer carpenters, employed nails, screws, and hand saws to assemble the frame of a 20-meter communal loghouse.

They anchored the heavy beams securely, layering the walls with triple-ply thermal insulation made from pine bark, dried moss, and straw to completely seal out the winter chill.

Brigade Three, the 'Hunting & Foraging Unit,' spearheaded by Elya and Borg, guided mixed parties of pine nomads and deer hunters to deploy deadfall traps and chop through river ice to catch fresh fish.

The combined expertise of two master trackers expanded their hunting range safely, channeling a steady supply of fresh fish and wild game into the fortress larder.

And Brigade Four, the 'Sanitation & Healthcare Unit,' directed by Dr. Nicha, oversaw wound dressings, boiled drinking water, and established downwind waste incineration pits to prevent epidemic outbreaks among the crowded population.

The sight of three formerly bitter enemy tribes—the Cliff Deer, the Pine Nomads, and the former Demon Fangs—heaving timber, hammering nails, and calling out in rhythmic coordination under Thame's direction took shape before their eyes.

This marked the dawn of the **'Horseshoe Valley Coalition'**—the very first united league upon the earth ten thousand years before Christ.

Limestone blocks and pine logs were ferried continuously into the pass. The double-log earth-filled ramparts were extended an additional twenty meters to encompass the expanding settlement.

Archaic tribal songs harmonized into the icy gusts, dissolving all remaining suspicion between the clans.

As dusk descended... the setting sun washed the limestone crags in crimson and amber gold. The rhythmic clang of steel hammers and warm laughter mingled with columns of woodsmoke ascending into the pale blue evening sky.

Thame climbed atop the roof of the ten-wheeler. The nocturnal breeze brushed against his cheeks as he reached into his heavy parka.

The .38 Special revolver in its holster remained completely at peace, all forty-three brass cartridges untouched in their loops. The marine flare gun at his hip retained two red distress cartridges.

The young man retrieved his smartphone to inspect its status as cold mist settled over the valley floor.

A single fragile bar of 2G signal flickered on the screen. And on the international war room forum, a brand-new pinned crimson announcement from prominent astrophysics and geology institutes appeared:

`[URGENT ANNOUNCEMENT]` *Severe ionospheric electromagnetic fluctuations detected over the Highland Mountain Pass rift... Commencing decisive long-range physical transmission test tomorrow!*

Thame looked from the glowing screen down to the valley below, where over a hundred people were laboring hand in hand to build their new home... The spark of human civilization was igniting. Yet what the modern world would send across the rift tomorrow—would it bring salvation, or an entirely new ordeal?
