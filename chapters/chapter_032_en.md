# Chapter 32: Steel Axes and the First Timber Cabins
Elya's corded arm snapped up without warning, barring Thame's chest as her fingers clicked the recurve bow into ready draw.
The huntress's primal instincts commanded her into an immediate low crouch, her keen eyes piercing the mist toward the dirt beyond the fence.
"Hold... do not take another step," Elya hissed in a harsh whisper, prompting Thame in his heavy down parka to drop low beside her.
The primeval huntress extended her bow tip toward an ominous anomaly the instant they cleared the outermost spiked deadfall trench.
Her sharp, wide eyes locked onto the damp laterite soil, which was already hardening under the unyielding grip of the frost.
Directly before her lay the colossal pugmark of an apex predator, spanning well over twenty-five centimeters across the frozen earth.
The massive paw print was pressed deep into the packed ground, testifying to a monstrous body mass exceeding three hundred kilograms.
Four distinct, dagger-sharp claw impressions gouged the soil, and on the trunk of a dead Scots pine ten paces beyond, deep furrows were torn into the bark.
These vertical gouge marks, raked clean at human chest height, revealed pale, fresh wood fibers and amber resin still weeping from the wound.
"Horok..." Elya whispered beneath her breath, her fingers instinctively unlimbering the composite recurve bow into a ready draw.
She pressed two fingers into the chilled soil and lifted them to her nostrils. "Scent is fresh... cold wind carries the musk... it prowled through just before dawn."
She pointed toward several coarse, golden-yellow guard hairs snagged tightly in the sticky resin along the claw torn bark.
"Mane hair... thick... born for brutal winter," Elya explained through crisp hand signs and primal hunter cadences.
"Horok marks its boundary... claims the forest... warns all intruders before the great snow falls."
"It came to reconnoiter our compound," Thame murmured, kneeling to gauge the depth of the paw prints with practical calculation.
"It heard the engine, heard our voices, and knows that a wealth of living prey gathers behind these wire barriers."
Inside the courtyard before the container doors, the rhythmic clatter of bone armor and spear shafts sounded the call to arms.
Kuran and Kraak marshaled thirty hardened warriors into an unbroken line, standing shoulder to shoulder against the whistling wind.
Encased in his steel-belted radial tire cuirass, Kraak tightened his grip around the heavy spiked war club, his fierce eyes flickering with grim respect.
"Horok... master of the dark woods!" Kraak grunted in guttural, staccato syllables forged in the fires of primal survival.
"Solitary stalker... ghost in the mist... fangs long as mammoth ivory... snaps the neck of giant beasts in a single crunch!"
Kuran stepped forward, the elder chieftain's face lined with grave caution. "The southern pine forest is its hunting ground... entering to cut timber invites slaughter."
"Yet we cannot halt the construction," Thame countered firmly, locking eyes with both chieftains with resolute determination.
"The great polar blizzards of the Younger Dryas will strike any day now. Without fifteen heavy timber cabins, the cold will kill us just as surely as any predator."
Dr. Nicha emerged from the field medical pavilion by the courtyard hearth, her sterile white coat donned over a thick thermal sweater.
Her discerning eyes swept across the gathered warriors before she handed Thame a vacuum flask filled with steaming pine needle tea.
"In dense mist like this, visibility inside the timberline drops to under thirty meters," Dr. Nicha cautioned with clinical gravity.
"A machairodont hunts through ambush from blind angles. If you enter the trees, Thame, you must form an airtight formation with overlapping sightlines."
Thame gave a solemn nod of assent. "No one breaks ranks, and no one walks alone as easy bait."
Turning toward the cavernous interior of the ten-wheel truck, he strode to the opened rear container doors and retrieved a sturdy carton from Zone One.
Printed crisply on the thick corrugated cardboard was the black tracking manifest `#TH-812049`, weighing twelve point eight kilograms.
The shipping label designated it as commercial timber framing and masonry tools, intended for an alpine construction site in the modern world.
Drawing his 1095 high-carbon fixed blade, Thame sliced through the reinforced packing tape, revealing the gleaming silver steel beneath.
Nestled inside were two heavy professional felling axes, their forged carbon steel heads polished and coated with industrial rust-inhibiting lacquer.
Their handles were crafted from high-visibility orange shock-absorbing fiberglass, ergonomic and wrapped with non-slip textured rubber grips.
Beside them lay two heavy-duty ten-meter steel tape measures, a magnetic aluminum torpedo spirit level,
whose fluorescent green vials glowed faintly, capturing the wide-eyed wonder of Kuran and Kraak.
"Eyes of the sun... measuring absolute truth... the air bubble balances earth and sky," Thame explained in swift, simple terms.
Beneath lay ten pairs of heavy-duty knit work gloves coated with thick, vulcanized textured rubber across the palms and fingers.
The dense orange rubber coating offered total protection against razor-sharp splinters while insulating flesh from the bitter chill of freezing steel.
Kuran slipped on a pair, flexing his gnarled fingers in sheer disbelief at the seamless grip and warm flexibility.
Thame distributed the work gloves to the logging leads, supplementing them with hand-forged axes and splitting wedges hammered from leaf spring steel (5160 Spring Steel).
"These are the iron talons that will fell the ancient forest," Thame declared, handing the first orange felling axe to Kraak and the second to Kuran.
Kraak ran his calloused thumb along the keen, mirror-honed edge, feeling the flawless balance that made his corded forearm muscles twitch with eager power.
"Beast fangs... razor-sharp... unbroken by rock!" Kraak roared, slamming his left fist against his armored chest in boundless pride.
Without delay, Thame organized the expedition into three tactical echelons designed to maintain total security within the predator's territory.
The first echelon was the scout and overwatch unit, led by Elya and her archers who ascended the perimeter crags and tall pine boughs.
The second echelon was the combat vanguard, commanded by Kraak and his armored warriors bristling with barbed-wire bamboo pikes, holding a twenty-meter perimeter ring.
The third echelon was the logging and transport detachment, spearheaded by Thame, Kuran, and twenty strapping men equipped with the steel tools.
The logging column filed purposefully through the double-walled stockade gate and the barbed-wire barriers, marching directly toward the ancient southern timberline.
Their footsteps crunched softly across the dry pine needle floor amidst an atmosphere taut with unspoken danger.
The air within the primeval taiga was glacial, heavy with the sharp aroma of pine turpentine, damp moss, and the frozen humus of millennia.
Thame approached a majestic Scots pine, its trunk straight as an arrow, measuring nearly two feet across and towering over twenty meters into the gray vault.
Snapping out the steel tape measure, he gauged the trunk's circumference before signaling Kraak and Kuran to flank the base.
"Observe the directional notch... chop downward at forty-five degrees... then flat along the horizon... dictating where the giant falls!" Thame demonstrated the mechanics.
Thwack!
The forged steel felling axe bit deep through the thick bark, burying itself into the fragrant sapwood as smoothly as a heated knife parting butter.
Golden-amber wood chips exploded in all directions, releasing an intoxicating burst of natural resin into the crisp air.
Kraak's jaw dropped at the staggering depth achieved in a single strike; the barbarian warlord bellowed in exhilaration, swinging his axe to match the undercut.
Clang! Thwack! Clang! Thwack!
Kuran took his turn with the forged spring steel axe on the opposite face, the high-carbon metal absorbing the shockwave without jarring his aged wrists.
The old chieftain gasped in awe as the dense heartwood yielded effortlessly to the keen edge, devoid of the shuddering recoil common to stone adzes.
The rhythmic, metallic cadence of steel biting timber reverberated across the limestone basin, shattering the primeval stillness of the ancient forest.
The industrial metallurgy outstripped stone tools ten times over, parting grain cleanly without chipping or dulling in the slightest.
In under fifteen minutes, the gaping wedge-shaped undercut had carved past the midpoint of the colossal trunk.
Thame directed Kuran to drive a leaf spring splitting wedge into the back-cut, applying mechanical leverage to tip the balance.
Crack... groaning crack... rumble!
The heartwood fibers tore apart in a deafening crescendo as the towering crown leaned inexorably along the planned trajectory.
"Heads up... tree falling... fall back to the rock line!" Thame shouted, waving his arms as the loggers scrambled to their safety zones.
Crashhhh!
The giant pine hammered the forest floor with concussive force, the earth trembling so violently that flocks of ancient grouse erupted from the undergrowth.
The warriors raised a triumphant roar, astonished that a giant of the wilderness could be brought low in mere minutes.
Wasting no time, Thame set the crew to work with folding hand saws and axes, shearing off branches until only a clean, straight log remained.
Using the steel splitting wedges and heavy mallets, they cross-cut and notched the timber into uniform four-meter and six-meter cabin lengths.
Eight muscular men levered the log upward with the heavy wrecking bar, slipping rounded pine poles beneath the trunk to serve as transport rollers.
The mechanical leverage of the steel crowbar combined with the rolling poles halved the physical strain required of the workers.
The immense pine trunk glided forward in steady rhythm, keeping time with the guttural work chants of the synchronized crew.
Kuran smeared animal tallow and slippery pine pitch across the roller poles, drastically cutting friction across the gravelly path.
Weighing hundreds of kilograms, the great log rolled smoothly toward the outpost without catching or dragging in the loam.
Yet just as the initial timber was clearing the tree line, the freezing wind shifted abruptly, swirling from the deep south.
A suffocating, predatory stench of stale blood and feral musk slammed into the nostrils of every man present.
Snap... crunch!
The distinct crack of a heavy branch sounded through the swirling mist, less than forty meters from Kraak's defensive perimeter.
Weeeeee-shriek!
High above on a limestone shelf, Elya's titanium survival whistle cut through the fog in a piercing, two-toned alert of mortal peril.
Kraak snapped his radial tire cuirass into alignment, bracing his nail-studded club across his chest, his unblinking eyes scouring the gloom.
Through the ghostly white haze, a massive, tawny-gold silhouette stalked into view from the shadows of the ancient pine stand.
It was the sovereign machairodont—the primeval Smilodon—stretching over two and a half meters in muscular, lethal length.
Its compact shoulders were mountainous slabs of living muscle, built with terrifying power to grapple prehistoric megafauna to the earth.
Curving downward from its upper jaw were twin ivory saber canines over twenty centimeters long, extending past its lower jaw in terrifying magnificence.
Glowing amber eyes burned with predatory intelligence, while a coarse crimson tongue rasped across its lips, baring bone-crushing carnassials.
It paced forward in dead silence, sinking low to the ground, coiled like an immense steel spring ready to launch a fatal pounce.
"Form the line... bristling fangs... hold fast!" Kraak roared, his warriors locking shields and leveling barbed-wire pikes forward in an impenetrable wall.
Thame calmly set his axe aside, unshouldering the 175-pound compound hunting crossbow and aligning the illuminated red dot dead between the beast's eyes.
He knew the forty-three rounds of .38 Special must remain sacred; the silent, lethal power of the compound limbs was his weapon of choice.
Up on the cliff edge, Elya drew her recurve bow to maximum draw, the carbon-steel broadhead aimed straight into the predator's right eye socket.
The Smilodon froze mid-stride, its amber gaze sweeping across the unbroken thicket of razor barbs, the cold gleam of steel, and the utter absence of panic.
The beast registered the unnatural, concentrated pressure of coordinated human intelligence; a deep, infrasonic growl vibrated through its chest like thunder beneath the earth.
It calculated the cost of assault... these were no longer scattering, hairless apes easy for the taking, but an armed pack bearing lethal iron fangs.
Slowly, deliberately, the sabertooth cat retreated step by step, before turning and vanishing into the swirling mist of the southern woods without a sound.
Absolute silence settled over the forest, broken only by the ragged, heavy exhalations of the disciplined warriors.
"It has withdrawn..." Kuran exhaled, his knuckles white, while Kraak lowered his war club and gazed upon Thame's crossbow with newfound reverence.
"Yet it will return when night falls," Thame stated calmly. "Let us haul every trunk back into the sanctuary at once!"
Their morale soaring, the men threw their collective weight into the rollers, felling five additional pines before retreating safely behind the stockade.
The eastern terrace flanking the limestone cliffs was transformed into a bustling construction site of historic proportions.
Thame drew out the steel tape measure, pegging four corner stakes into an exact four-by-six-meter rectangle aligned to precise geometry.
Kuran led ten men wielding the forged spring steel mattock hoes, excavating the stony ground to a depth of fifty centimeters for a semi-subterranean foundation.
This sunken earth floor provided natural geothermal insulation, keeping the bitter above-ground winds from chilling the cabin's lower perimeter.
Thame laid the magnetic spirit level across the bed of crushed limestone gravel, the luminous vial bubble resting dead-center to verify true level.
Before laying the timber courses, Thame demonstrated a vital bushcraft discipline: thorough debarking of every pine trunk.
The young engineer wielded a steel brush hook and forged axe to strip away the coarse outer bark and brown cambium down to the bare, silky white wood.

Kuran rendered fragrant pine resin with rendered animal tallow over the fire, brushing the thick sealant onto the cut log ends to prevent radial checking.

Along the longitudinal grooves flanking each course, workers tamped thick beds of dried sphagnum moss and pine needles as primary thermal chinking.
They then daubed the seams with a dense refractory mortar of sticky clay, sharp sand, and fine limestone powder, shutting out sub-zero draughts completely.
The massive sill logs rested squarely upon the leveled gravel trenches, anchoring a semi-subterranean fortress as sturdy as the limestone cliffs themselves.

The engine of this system was a classic J-tube combustion core, sculpted from heat-resistant limestone blocks and refractory clay cob.
A compact vertical fuel drop tube fed into a narrow horizontal combustion tunnel, which terminated into an insulated vertical heat riser.
This vertical heat riser was shrouded in a ten-centimeter sleeve of volcanic ash and river sand, driving internal temperatures past one thousand degrees Celsius.
This infernal heat triggered hyper-efficient secondary combustion, vaporizing volatile wood gases and tar completely, turning dark smoke into pure heat.
Over this glowing riser, Thame lowered an inverted steel heat exchange drum adapted from salvaged truck body plating.
The searing exhaust gases struck the steel crown, radiating an immediate surge of gentle warmth into the cabin atmosphere within minutes,
before cooling to roughly one hundred and fifty degrees Celsius and being drawn downward into horizontal earthenware sub-floor conduits.
This winding underfloor flue snaked across a four-meter run, built of heavy baked clay ducting encased within a massive limestone masonry bench.
Dr. Nicha formulated the ideal cob mortar ratio of clay, sand, and wood ash to sculpt a smooth, durable exterior across the masonry bench.
Weighing over two tons, this immense mass of stone and cob acted as a natural thermal battery, absorbing nearly all residual heat from the exhaust.
By the time the spent gases reached the exterior chimney vent, their temperature had plummeted to a mere forty degrees Celsius.
The system harvested over ninety percent of the fuel's thermal energy, burning five times less firewood than an open prehistoric campfire.
