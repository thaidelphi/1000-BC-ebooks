# Chapter 33: Fangs in the Mist and the Cliffside Crucible
A dense, leaden mist rolled down through the limestone clefts like a silent wave of glacial ice.
Frigid moisture condensed into a crust of white rime, coating the corrugated steel plates of the ten-wheeler and ancient pine boughs.
The temperature plunged swiftly toward two degrees Celsius, numbing cheeks and fingertips with biting cold.
A sudden gust swept the pungent, musky stench of an ancient apex predator across the cliff base with unnatural sharpness.
The wildlife of the taiga had fallen dead silent; not even the horned owls uttered a hunting cry.
The oppressive quiet hung heavy over the camp—the foremost warning sign of the Pleistocene wilderness.
In the midst of heavy carpentry and stone dressing, Thame wore only a thick flannel shirt and a heavy leather work apron.
At his belt hung only pliers and a bushcraft knife, while his 175-pound hunting crossbow stood propped against a nearby stone bench for security.
Grak and three broad-shouldered hunters were hauling a massive taiga pine trunk weighing over five hundred kilograms with paracord lines.
The six-meter timber slid grinding across the limestone bedrock toward the pylon sockets quarried for the cliff ascent.
Heavy steam plumed from the corded, sweat-sheened backs of the warriors against the freezing gale.
Thame knelt with a spirit level and plumb line, checking the alignment of the cliff-base bedrock channel he had just dressed.
Suddenly, the sharp crack of snapping dry pine branches sounded on the left flank, less than fifteen paces away behind the dense fog.
Instinct surged like an electric shock, hair bristling on Thame's arms before his eyes even caught the shifting silhouette.
"Take cover!" Thame roared, his voice echoing off the limestone crag in warning.
A colossal tawny-gold silhouette erupted from the mist like a collapsing mountainside.
A fully grown Smilodon—a primeval saber-toothed cat packing over three hundred kilograms of savage muscle.
Its crest and shoulder girdle bulged like cast iron, its golden pelt caked with dried mud and frozen silt.
Two flattened, curved canines over twenty centimeters long protruded past its jowls, gleaming like ancient short swords.
Amber eyes blazed with the ferocious madness of a starving apex predator.
The target of its charge was not the timber pile, but Torg, the teenage hunter pulling at the rear of the haul line.
The beast pounced with terrifying velocity, a predatory blur defying human reflexes.
Panicked, Torg stumbled over a limestone ledge, the haul line slipping from his frozen hands.
Stripped of its counter-tension, the half-ton pine log broke free and tumbled down the slope with crushing inertia.
The massive timber slammed across Torg's right lower leg, pinning the youth against the rock wall.
The sickening crunch of splintering bone was swallowed by screams as hot blood sprayed across the frosted stone.
The stench of fresh blood drove the saber-toothed monster into an uncontrollable predatory frenzy.
Dropping its haunches, it gaped its jaws to sink eight-inch sabers into Torg's throat for an instant kill.
"OROK!" Grak bellowed like thunder, dropping his line and hurling his massive frame forward barehanded.
The primal champion swung a fire-hardened hardwood stake across the beast's snout with all his might.
The timber snapped upon impact before a massive clawed paw swept back, slamming square into Grak's chest.
The crushing blow hurled Grak's hulking frame through the air, crashing against the rocks as blood sprayed from his mouth.
Though his steel-belted tire armor absorbed the lethal claw stroke, the talons shredded the tread and inflicted severe blunt trauma.
The predator did not break stride; it wheeled about, baring yellowed fangs to spring upon the helpless youth once more.
In that heartbeat of life and death, Thame snatched the pre-cocked hunting crossbow from the stone bench to his shoulder.
At a distance of barely eight paces, the carbon bolt locked onto the beast's bulging chest without hesitation.
The limbs released with a sharp hum, driving the high-carbon steel broadhead deep into the predator's front shoulder muscle.
A deafening howl of agony tore from the beast as it staggered back two paces, dark arterial blood gushing from the wound.
Before it could recover, a flint-tipped arrow coated in toxic pine resin from Elya's recurve bow buried itself beneath its left ear.
The predator thrashed its head in berserk fury, amber eyes locking onto Thame and Elya with venomous wrath.
It knew instantly that the bipeds before it possessed lethal fangs of their own.
Yet the beast's ferocity refused to break; with an earth-shaking snarl, it lunged forward, sweeping razor talons to tear Thame apart.
Discarding the spent crossbow, Thame gripped the three-point-five-kilogram rescue crowbar lying at his feet in both hands.
Slipping beneath the whistling talons by inches, he threw his entire weight into a savage swing, driving the steel chisel into the beast's wounded shoulder.
Hardened steel collided with dense muscle and bone, the violent impact jarring the three-hundred-kilogram monster sideways.
Before it could regain its footing, Thame drove a follow-up strike into its jaw, shattering incisors in a spray of blood.
At that exact moment, Grak surged to his feet and hurled a thirty-kilogram limestone boulder into the cat's exposed flank.
Elya loosed a second shaft that buried itself deep into its hip with lethal precision.
Battered by shattered bone and grievous trauma, primal survival instinct finally overrode the predator's bloodlust.
Springing backward toward the ravine rim, the wounded beast hissed venomously, blood pouring from its shoulder and shattered maw.
Its amber eyes burned with lingering malice before it turned and vanished like a wraith into the freezing mist.
"Torg! Get Torg out!" Elya shouted, rushing toward the young hunter who was drifting toward unconsciousness.
Half a ton of pine still pinned Torg's crushed limb; the boy's face was ashen, lips turning blue from profound shock.
Rapid footsteps pounded through the freezing fog as Dr. Nicha, alerted by the commotion, sprinted from the medical tent with her field kit.
"Thame! Don't pull him out directly!" Dr. Nicha commanded urgently as she dropped beside the casualty.
"Watch for crush toxins and arterial tears! If a major artery is ruptured, lifting the log suddenly will cause fatal circulatory collapse!"
Thame nodded sharply. "Grak! Help me pry! Kuran, roll that thick limestone block under here!"
Thame drove the chisel tip of the rescue crowbar beneath the pine log, setting the limestone rock as a secure fulcrum.
Grak and two hunters threw their full body weight onto the crowbar's hexagonal shank with all their might.
Mechanical leverage hoisted the five-hundred-kilogram trunk clear of Torg's crushed limb.
Elya and Thame drew Torg backward onto an insulating sheepskin pallet with meticulous care.
Opening her field kit, Dr. Nicha's hands were steady as her fingers swiftly palpated the dorsalis pedis pulse.
"Dorsal pedal pulse is present—the main artery didn't sever!" Dr. Nicha exhaled with fierce relief.
She irrigated the compound laceration with sterile saline, packing sterile gauze and hemostatic powder to arrest the bleeding.
"Splint the fracture now, hurry!"
Thame aligned two straight pine splints along Torg's right leg, binding them rigid with taut coils of paracord.
"He is in deep shock and fading—no water or pills out here, he will aspirate!" Dr. Nicha warned sharply.
"Litter him into the container now. Wrap him in a foil blanket to stop hypothermia before we reduce and medicate!"
Four hunters lifted the canvas litter, bearing Torg swiftly into the warmth of the steel container.
Inside the steel shelter, the air was warm and stable, insulated against the freezing draft by wood stove heat.
Dr. Nicha had them lay Torg on a fur pallet before shears cut away the buckskin trousers to inspect the displaced tibial deformity.
Torg groaned back to consciousness from the piercing agony, cold sweat beading on his soot-streaked brow.
As his gaze fell upon his twisted, unnatural shin, the young hunter's eyes widened in terror and sheer despair.
Tears welled in the warrior's eyes; with trembling fingers, he clutched Thame's sleeve in frantic dread.
"Thame... Torg... broken..." his voice broke into a ragged whisper, pointing toward the snow outside. "Leave... cold... die?"
In the unforgiving Ice Age, a hunter with a fractured limb was dead weight, invariably abandoned to the frost.
"Never!" Thame leaned down and gripped Torg's shoulder firmly with immovable intensity. "This is Fortress Alpha. We never leave our people behind!"
"Your bone will heal true. You will walk, hunt, and run the ridges again!"
Elder Kuran stepped beside the pallet, pressing a calloused palm against Torg's forehead in the clan sign of peaceful brotherhood.
"Sua... fight!" the old hunter grunted with a voice as steady as bedrock.
Torg bit down hard on a cloth-wrapped wooden bite-stick, neck cords bulging as Dr. Nicha and Thame prepared the reduction.
Thame anchored the knee firmly while Dr. Nicha applied steady, decisive axial traction along the ankle.
The displaced tibial ends clicked cleanly back into anatomical alignment; Torg shuddered violently before exhaling a long breath of relief.
Dr. Nicha cleansed the wound with antiseptic, sutured the laceration shut, and immobilized the leg between pine splints wrapped in duct tape.
Once Torg regained clear consciousness and could swallow safely, Dr. Nicha administered two Amoxicillin tablets and an Ibuprofen with warm salted broth.
A silver reflective space blanket was wrapped snugly around him to lock in his core body temperature.
Torg looked down at his mended limb, tears of gratitude and fierce devotion tracing tracks through the soot on his cheeks.
The hunters gathered at the open doors murmured in awe, their primal spirits struck with profound reverence.
This healing craft—and a leader who refused to abandon the broken—forged an unbreakable bond between primitive humanity and their new world.
Thame gave Torg's shoulder a gentle pat, then turned and stepped back into the freezing cold outside with purposeful resolve.
The brush with death stripped away all lingering complacency across the camp.
Blood still darkened the limestone ground, a beacon that would draw every scavenger in the valley by nightfall.
Originally, the ascent to the hidden cliff cave fifteen meters above relied solely on a steep karst fissure and mountain goat ledges.
The clan had scrambled up this treacherous route using wild vines and notched pine poles.
Yet the natural incline was gentle enough for agile predators like the Smilodon to scramble up with ease.
Thame's plan was to sever this vulnerability permanently by demolishing the old ramp and installing a defensive drawbridge.
"Grak! Sledge away the lower footholds—demolish the ramp!" Thame commanded.
Grak and the warriors battered the lower three meters of limestone ledges with pry-bars and iron hammers until they crumbled.
Rock fragments tumbled down, transforming the lower cliff into a sheer, slick vertical face stripped clean of all claw-holds.
From the organized tool racks inside the cargo container, Thame retrieved the heavy hand-brace auger and one-inch spiral bits.
Beside it sat a wooden keg containing ten kilograms of six-inch galvanized boat nails staged for heavy timber framing.
Taking his station at the cliff, Thame pressed the breastplate of the auger against his chest and turned the crank rhythmically.
The spiral flutes bit deep, showering aromatic ribbons of golden pine shavings onto the rock.
Clean, one-inch cylindrical mortise holes were bored through the dense pine logs in a matter of minutes.
Grak drove seasoned oak trunnels home with a heavy mallet, locking the cross-girders flush to the main uprights.
Then followed the thunderous driving of six-inch galvanized boat nails into the heartwood, locking the pylons rigid as bedrock.
The twin vertical pylons were hoisted erect, seating firmly into the quarried stone sockets.
Hunters packed crushed limestone, gravel, and hydraulic clay around the bases until they stood immovable.
The three-meter lower drawbridge was assembled from split logs fastened across heavy timber stringers.
The timber deck was hinged upon massive oak pivot pins set at the junction where the upper rock path began.
Heavy paracord was reeved through overhead pulleys on the crossbeam, counterbalanced by an eighty-kilogram limestone block.
In daylight, the timber bridge spanned the chiseled three-meter gap, allowing safe passage up to the cliff trail.
But when Thame tripped the release line, the counterweight hoisted the wooden ramp vertically, severing ground access in an instant.
The limestone face below became an unbroken sheer wall, cutting off all ground-level approach.
No beast could leap across the three-meter void to breach the sanctuary above.
Kuran and the hunters gazed upon the counterweight drawbridge with fierce wonder and newfound security.
Dusk fell swiftly, swallowing the valley as a biting northern wind plunged the mercury to zero degrees.
Upon the rock ledge above the drawbridge, Thame and Elya stood sentinel with bow and crossbow at the ready.
The LED headlamp beam cut through the swirling fog, sweeping the perimeter below.
Across the dusting of snow before the wire fence, a trail of dark blood led into the pitch-black southern pines.
The freezing gale carried a deep, resonant roar vibrating up from the valley floor.
It was not the solitary cry of a wounded cat.
It was the chorus of answering roars from a pack coordinating in the dark.
And deep within the timber, the rhythmic, guttural blast of a human horn answered the beasts.
Elya gripped her bow, her body taut with tension as she whispered with primal instinct.
"Orok... Demon Fangs... blow beast horn... they drive the fangs!"
Dr. Nicha stepped outside the container, her face pale with alarm.
"Thame! The thermal sensors on the truck roof are flashing—dozens of hot signatures moving through the southern pines!"
"They aren't just scavenging... they are encircling Fortress Alpha!"
Thame signaled the hunters along the rock ledge to ignite pine-pitch flares along the razor-wire perimeter.
Leaping orange flames pushed back the gloom, illuminating the massive silhouettes of predators prowling at the wire.
Kuran and Grak locked ranks behind the drawbridge platform, steel-tipped spears and forged axes held ready.
Thame stared into the gloom of the taiga, his hands tightening around his rescue crowbar and crossbow.
Fortress Alpha was forged of blood, stone, and tempered steel.
And tonight, the legacy of modern civilization would face its trial by fire against the beasts of the ice age!
