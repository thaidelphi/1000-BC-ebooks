# Chapter 7: The Carbon Steel Pact

A chilling, deathly stillness descended over the limestone canyon the moment the pillar of pitch-black smoke in the south pierced the blood-red twilight sky.

The chilling gale whistled through jagged rock fissures, carrying with it the unmistakable acrid stench of incinerated pine encampments and the faint, coppery tang of spilled blood.

Kuran stood frozen beside the rear boarding ladder of the ten-wheeler, the weathered chieftain’s sinewy frame standing resolute against the freezing blast.

His gaze locked onto the horizon ablaze with fire and soot, veins throbbing at his temples with raw fury. His calloused hands, rough as petrified bark, gripped the haft of his flint-tipped spear until his knuckles blanched white.

"Kraak..." Kuran rasped from deep within his chest, his eyes burning with a venomous mixture of ancient hatred and mortal dread that Thame had never witnessed before.

The chieftain spun toward Thame, unleashing a frantic sequence of physical signs. He jabbed a trembling finger at the smoke column, followed by a vicious throat-slitting motion and a crushing hammer fist against his own temple.

He dropped to one knee on the frozen muck, using the flint spearhead to furiously scratch crude figures onto the frost-crusted ground.

Kuran’s sketch depicted towering, broad-shouldered figures crowned with ferocious beast skulls sporting elongated, curved fangs and jagged horns.

They wielded colossal stone war-hammers and spears tipped with human bone, encircling shelters in flames and dragging the limp bodies of women and children across the crimson snow.

Thame stood with arms folded, thick brows furrowed as plumes of white steam billowed from his lips in the sub-zero chill.

He understood the message instantly without needing an interpreter.

The Beast Fang Tribe under warlord Kraak were not mere nomadic foragers.

They were an organized, cannibalistic warmongering horde sweeping the foothills—slaughtering, pillaging winter caches, and harvesting captives for slavery or ritual consumption before the great ice storm descended.

Kuran pointed toward a limestone cliff terrace roughly two kilometers away, the ancestral shelter caves of the Cliff Deer Tribe.

Then the old leader shook his head in bitter despair. Their caves were porous limestone hollows with multiple wide, exposed openings.

Freezing winds tore straight through them, completely lacking stone ramparts or natural chokepoints. If Kraak’s war party fell upon them, all thirty members of the clan would be butchered without hope of escape.

Kuran turned his pleading eyes back toward the vibrant orange twenty-five-ton cargo truck, his gaze overflowing with desperate reverence.

He pointed urgently to the ten massive black rubber wheels—which looked like the monstrous paws of a sleeping titan—then toward the distant ridges, frantically pleading for Thame to "awaken" the iron beast and command it to walk across the valley to save his clan.

Thame released a heavy sigh and slowly shook his head.

He guided Kuran around to the truck’s undercarriage, crouching down to point into the twisted wreckage beneath the reinforced steel chassis.

The dual front multi-leaf springs and rear bogie suspension were snapped in half, the central heavy-duty driveshaft twisted like a pretzel, and the shredded radial tires were sunk deep into the frozen moraine mud.

"Can't move, old man..." Thame said in a low, measured voice, spreading his arms wide before slapping the cold, rigid corrugated steel. "It doesn't move. It cannot run... It is a mountain. It is a fortress."

Kuran froze. A veil of profound grief and defeat washed over his rugged features.

He realized this iron god could not march across the crags to save his kin.

Yet Thame reached out, his gloved hand coming down firmly on the chieftain’s muscular shoulder, meeting the elder’s gaze with unyielding, steely resolve.

Thame pointed at Kuran, pointed toward the distant caves, then swept his arm in a wide, sweeping perimeter circling the truck before tapping his own chest.

"Bring your people here," Thame articulated with deliberate weight.

"Move the entire Cliff Deer clan to camp around this truck. These thick steel walls will break the arctic wind for you. And if those Beast Fang bastards dare march here... we fight together."

Kuran’s breath caught in his throat, his chest hitching as his heart pounded against his ribs.

To shelter thirty lives beneath the looming shadow of the iron titan and the master of lightning and sacred fire was a covenant of survival unprecedented in ancestral memory.

The old chieftain dropped to one knee, bowing his head against his chest before pressing his calloused right palm flat against Thame’s open hand.

It was a blood oath forged between two civilizations separated by ten thousand years, bound without a single written word on paper.

"But before they arrive, we need weapons and fortifications," Thame muttered to himself.

To fortify a defensive perimeter and process raw materials before nightfall, brittle knapped flint flakes would never suffice. He needed modern industrial hand tools from the cargo hold.

Thame climbed into the container, sweeping his tactical LED flashlight across the racks until his beam settled on a heavy fiber-reinforced corrugated carton bearing tracking barcode `#TH-662301`.

The thermal waybill was unmistakable: *Industrial Contractor Heavy-Duty Cutting & Fastening Kit, Weight: 3.2 kg.*

"Forgive me once again, owner... but thirty human lives depend on this tonight," Thame whispered, pressing his palms together in customary apology before slicing the reinforced tape with his utility knife.

The contents made the young automotive engineer grin with profound relief:

- **Heavy-Duty Zinc-Alloy Industrial Utility Knives (SK5 Box Cutters)** with ergonomic rubberized grip and positive steel ratchet lock (2 units)
- **High-Carbon SK5 Alloy Replacement Blades** with rust-resistant oil coating in safety dispensers (2 packs of 10, total 20 blades)
- **UV-Resistant Heavy-Duty Nylon Cable Ties (300mm x 7.6mm)** rated for 55 kg tensile load (1 pack of 100 ties)
- **Heavy-Duty Industrial Silver Cloth Duct Tape**, 2-inch width, 50-meter rolls (2 rolls)

To a resident of the twenty-first century, it was mundane hardware store inventory.

In 10,000 BC, it was game-changing tactical infrastructure!

Thame grabbed one silver SK5 cutter and a roll of industrial duct tape and stepped back down to the ground.

Kuran and shaman Moga edged closer, watching with wary fascination.

Thame pressed his thumb against the ribbed slider and pushed forward. The mechanical ratchet mechanism clicked crisply in the cold silence:

*Clack... clack... clack!*

A trapezoidal, razor-edged high-carbon blade gleamed silver under the twilight as it extended precisely from the zinc housing.

Moga staggered back two steps, his gnarled hand trembling as he uttered a fearful chant. To the old shaman, this was a demon’s steel claw that retracted and struck on command!

Wasting no time, Thame grabbed a stiff, dried bison hide—thick as plywood—which Kuran had spent hours sawing with flint to carve spear straps.

Thame laid the hide over an ash log, set the SK5 blade against the grain, and pulled back smoothly.

*Ssshhhk!*

The stubborn leather parted into two cleanly separated strips as effortlessly as wet paper, the high-carbon edge severing dense collagen fibers without the slightest resistance.

Kuran’s eyes nearly bulged from their sockets. The chieftain dropped to his knees, his trembling fingers tracing the hair-thin cut in sheer disbelief.

The knapped flint tools his ancestors had honed for millennia became dull pebbles in the shadow of this "fang of the gods."

Next, Thame retrieved an ash spear belonging to the young hunter, its socket split and splintered from the struggle with the cave bear.

Traditional repairs required soaking animal sinew in water, wrapping it painstakingly, and waiting days for it to shrink and cure.

Thame threaded two heavy-duty black cable ties around the cracked socket, pulling them taut with a sharp hiss:

*Zzzzzzt... click!*

The nylon teeth locked into place, clamping the split wood shut under fifty-five kilograms of pressure. Thame then wrapped three layers of reinforced silver duct tape over the joint, slicing the tape cleanly with the box cutter.

The entire repair took less than thirty seconds!

Thame thrust the spear back into Kuran’s hands.

The chieftain tested the shaft, slamming it hard against a limestone boulder. The repaired collar remained rock-solid, completely rigid without a creak or wobble.

"Miraculous..." Moga fell prostrate, worshipping the silver tape roll and the cutter handle with unreserved awe.

"Go gather your people, Kuran, before complete darkness falls," Thame said, clapping the chieftain’s shoulder and handing him the second SK5 cutter loaded with a fresh blade.

Kuran took the silver tool with trembling hands, treating it like a sacred scepter of chieftain authority. He bowed deeply before sprinting into the gloom alongside his young hunter.

With Kuran gone, Thame climbed back into the container to inspect El-ya.

The young huntress was awake. Her amber eyes were weary, but remarkably clear.

The septic fever had receded, her flushed skin cooling to normal temperature, and the angry redness around her thigh was noticeably subsiding. The Betadine gauze pack was drawing out the dark serosanguinous drainage perfectly.

Seeing Thame enter, El-ya attempted to push herself up, but Thame gently placed a hand on her shoulder, urging her back down. "Stay still. The wound is still draining."

He retrieved **one 500mg Amoxicillin capsule**—the scheduled second maintenance dose to preserve bactericidal plasma concentration—and poured warm, sterile boiled water from the vacuum thermos into the cap.

El-ya took the capsule into her mouth and swallowed the water obediently, devoid of fear or suspicion.

Her hunting fingers reached out, lightly brushing the cuff of Thame’s down parka, her cracked lips curving into a faint, poignant smile of complete trust.

Hours slipped past until the cold night enveloped the valley, the temperature dropping sharply to around seven or eight degrees Celsius. Although it was not yet the punishing sub-zero freeze of the Great Winter due to strike in four to five months, for Paleolithic villagers clad only in thin scraps of cured deer hide, the biting nocturnal chill was more than enough to set their teeth chattering uncontrollably.

Muffled shuffling footsteps and ragged breathing drifted up from the dark basin below.

The migration of the Cliff Deer Tribe had arrived!

Thirty exhausted souls trudged through the frost onto the stone terrace encircling the truck.

Mothers carried swaddled infants against their bare chests, elders leaned heavily on gnarly walking sticks, and children shivered violently from the biting cold. They gazed up at the orange steel leviathan in hushed terror and reverence.

Among them stumbled a gaunt boy around seven or eight years old, clad in shredded deer pelts, hollow-cheeked with starvation. His teeth chattered uncontrollably.

Thame looked upon the child with deep empathy, reminded of his own bleak days of hunger in a cramped city apartment.

He knelt before the boy, pulling out a packet of butter crackers from carton `#TH-204192`.

He broke off a golden, fragrant biscuit and offered it to the shivering child. "Eat... little one."

The boy looked toward his uncle, Kuran. At the chieftain’s nod, small soot-stained fingers took the cracker tentatively and brought it to his lips.

The moment teeth crunched into the golden biscuit, the rich dairy butter, salt, and gentle sweetness exploded across the starving boy’s palate.

His wide dark eyes flared with uncontainable light. His trembling body stilled. Warm tears carved pale tracks down his dirt-streaked cheeks as he chewed in profound bliss, tasting what could only be food of the heavens.

"Tara..." Kuran murmured softly, introducing the boy to Thame.

Little Tara swallowed the last crumb, then reached deep into his small leather pouch.

He brought out a water-polished black moraine river stone carved with crude antler markings.

It was his "lucky talisman"—the sole keepsake left by his deceased parents. Tara placed the stone into Thame’s hands with both palms as an offering of gratitude.

Thame accepted the polished stone, feeling the pure, radiant warmth of the child’s spirit.

He ruffled Tara’s dark hair gently, then carefully slid the lucky talisman into the left chest pocket of his parka... directly over his heart.

Around the ten-wheeled fortress, the clan began arranging dry pine branches into small, sheltered campfires along the massive wheels and under the container overhang.

The warmth of the fires and the impenetrable windbreak of the steel walls brought peaceful smiles and quiet solace to the tribe for the first time in months.

Yet... amidst this fragile sanctuary and the dawn of a new alliance.

High upon the jagged limestone crags towering three hundred meters above, two crouching figures melted into the shadows of the frost-shattered ridge.

Both scouts wore hollowed ice-age dire wolf skulls over their heads, their faces smeared with dried crimson ochre. Feral amber eyes bored unblinkingly down at the fires flickering around the orange steel beast.

The Beast Fang scouts had located their prey and the iron monster!

And they were turning back to inform warlord Kraak... to march their horde for total annihilation!
