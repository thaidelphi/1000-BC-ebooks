# Chapter 17: Into the Paleolithic Wild & Campfire Bonding

The biting glacial gales of the Younger Dryas whipped crystalline ice flurries through the jagged limestone chasms, their piercing shrieks echoing like the mournful cries of primordial spirits.

Behind Thame and Elya, the ramparts of Fortress Alpha and the warm orange beacons powered by the new hydro-turbine vanished behind towering karst crags.

Stretching endlessly before them lay the vast, primordial taiga forest of ten thousand years ago—a wilderness of colossal ancient pines reaching toward the heavens, draped in snow and silver-gray lichen.

Each prehistoric pine measured nearly two meters across its trunk, its deeply grooved bark exuding a sharp, pungent pine resin that permeated the frigid air.

Thame's Vibram-lugged trekking boots bit securely into slippery scree and glazed ice, granting him balanced, unyielding traction across the treacherous, snowbound slopes.

The 65-liter tactical expedition backpack riding on his back weighed around eighteen kilograms, packed with smoked jerky, a titanium mess kit, a vacuum flask, a down sleeping bag, 550 paracord, and trauma medical supplies.

Ahead of him, Elya stalked forward with the fluid grace of an apex feline, carrying her recurve bow in her left hand and the quiver of armor-piercing carbon arrows across her shoulders.

Abruptly, the huntress froze in her tracks, throwing up her right palm in the sharp signal for "Tah!" (Stop!) while dropping low against a moss-covered boulder.

Thame halted instantly, his fingertips instinctively brushing the grips of the .38 revolver at his hip as his eyes swept the expanse ahead.

Across the pristine snowfield stretching between two sheer granite walls, no predator tracks or signs of violence were visible—only unbroken, seemingly harmless powdery snow.

Yet Elya's weathered features tightened, her amber eyes narrowing as she scrutinized the snowy flatland as though gazing into a lethal trap.

"Mor... soul-swallowing void," Elya whispered, pointing toward the level snow ahead.

She plucked a brittle pine branch and cast it gently forward; the branch struck the crust and tilted unnaturally, vanishing beneath an invisible hollow.

Thame grasped the peril at once—beneath that smooth, innocent snow crust lay a hollow, air-filled abyss.

Unclipping a lightweight aluminum trekking pole from the side of his pack, Thame twisted the locking cams tight, stepped forward with care, and drove the carbide tip downward with full force.

*Thump! CRUUUUNCH!*

Under the impact of the pole, the paper-thin snow bridge collapsed with a terrifying shudder!

Crumbled blocks of packed snow and brittle ice bridges cascaded downward into the subterranean darkness, exposing a hidden glacial crevasse over fifteen meters deep!

The jagged chasm floor below bristled with razor-sharp ice stalagmites like punji stakes, while freezing vapor billowed up from the depths like the icy breath of death itself.

Without the probe pole, a single misplaced step would have sent either of them plummeting to be shattered against the jagged ice below.

Thame sucked a deep breath of freezing air into his lungs, beads of sweat breaking along his hairline despite the subzero chill. "That was close... a treacherous snow bridge."

Elya touched Thame's forearm gently, her gaze brimming with reverent awe for the metallic rod that could gauge the treacherous depths of the earth.

Circumventing the yawning crevasse, they navigated solid granite outcrops until they descended into a steep gorge bisected by a torrential river.

This was near-freezing meltwater rushing from high alpine glaciers, turbulent and crystal-clear, revealing smooth river stones beneath its surface at an estimated zero degrees Celsius.

Frigid spray tossed into the air sent violent shivers across exposed skin.

"We must cross here... the iron beast's trail lies on the far bank," Elya pointed across the ten-meter-wide torrent.

Thame knelt, dipping a bare fingertip into the water for a split second; the bone-numbing cold shot up his arm, leaving his finger numb and unresponsive.

"With water this cold, simply wading across will trigger violent leg cramps and sweep us downstream in seconds," Thame analyzed swiftly.

Slipping off his pack, he drew out fifty meters of 550-paracord and two steel carabiners.

Tying one end around a hefty, rounded throwing stone, he handed it to Elya: "Cast it across... wrap it three times around that pine trunk on the far bank!"

Elya grasped his intent immediately; whirling the stone in a whistling arc, she hurled it across the torrent, letting the weighted cord lash around a massive pine trunk three times until friction locked it solid.

Thame hauled the cord taut, anchoring the near end around an immovable granite boulder to establish a tensioned traverse line.

"I go first... hold the line tight, keep your feet up!" Thame instructed, punctuating his words with clear gestures.

Stepping into the raging torrent, the excruciating chill pierced his trekking trousers, causing his calf muscles to violently spasm.

Gritting his teeth, Thame locked both hands around the paracord line, using upper-body strength and his trekking pole to brace against the churning current until he hauled himself onto the opposite bank.

He signaled back to Elya; grasping the paracord, she waded through with resolute courage, even as her lips turned a pale, cyanotic blue from the icy shock.

Once both were safely ashore, Thame quickly pulled a clean, dry microfiber towel from his pack to dry her shivering legs, immediately helping her into spare merino wool socks to ward off frostbite.

Their trek through the prehistoric taiga continued throughout the afternoon until the sun began sinking behind the western crags, turning the sky deep twilight purple as the mercury plunged to minus eight degrees Celsius (-8°C).

They chose to make camp beneath a sprawling overhang of limestone rock shielded by a dense thicket of pines.

Beneath the overhang, the ground was bone-dry and carpeted with a thick bedding of fallen pine needles—an ideal natural bivouac.

As primordial night descended, an eerie, profound silence blanketed the wilderness, punctuated only by the distant, haunting howls of unseen carnivores drifting on the mountain breeze.

Thame reached for his magnesium rod and ferro striker, but Elya offered a faint, knowing smile and knelt beside a mound of dry kindling.

Taking a small primitive wooden bow drill and a hardwood spindle, she spun the shaft against a dry hearth board with practiced, astonishing speed.

In less than two minutes, a wisp of fragrant white smoke curled upward; Elya blew gently into a nest of dry tinder until a bright orange flame blossomed into life.

Thame watched in genuine admiration; prehistoric humans did not always require the modern magic of a jet lighter—theirs was the enduring art of true survival.

The campfire crackled robustly, casting radiant heat that banished the biting cold from the shelter of the overhang.

Thame unpacked the titanium mess kit, filled it with pristine creek water, and set it upon the flat stones flanking the hearth.

He diced chunks of smoked jerky into the simmering pot, dropped in half a cube of concentrated beef bouillon, and sprinkled a pinch of sea salt.

The rich, savory aroma of hot meat stew filled the rock hollow, making their mouths water in the bitter cold.

Once the stew reached a rolling boil, Thame ladled a steaming portion into a titanium cup and handed it to Elya.

The huntress blew softly on the silver-gray metal cup before taking an initial sip.

Her amber eyes widened in sheer bliss as the rich broth and restorative sodium warmed her throat and radiated through her chilled limbs.

"Loom... warmer than the sun itself," Elya murmured with a rare, genuine smile.

Next, Thame emptied a packet of instant ground coffee into a cup of boiling water, releasing its distinctive, roasted aroma into the cold night air.

He offered the steaming brew to Elya; she grimaced slightly at the initial bitterness, but as the warmth and stimulant coursed into her bloodstream, she nodded in appreciative wonder of the exotic drink.

They sat side by side beside the hearth, pine embers snapping into sparks that drifted toward the pitch-black sky.

High above the rocky crags arched the untainted heavens of ten thousand years ago, where millions of brilliant stars glittered like scattered diamond dust across a velvet tapestry.

The Milky Way stretched across the zenith like a luminous celestial river of pure molten silver.

Pointing upward, Thame guided Elya's eyes toward the Big Dipper and the steady, unflickering point of the North Star.

"That star... never wanders," Thame said in mixed Thai and ancient tribal tongue. "It guides lost travelers home, no matter how many millennia slip away."

Elya tilted her head, listening intently to his calm, deep cadence, before resting her palm against the forest floor beside the fire.

"Earth... wind... stars," Elya spoke softly, tuning her ears to the nocturnal wilderness. "When wind blows from the north, grass dries and beasts hide... but when the gale carries the scent of blood, hunters stalk downwind."

She taught Thame how to distinguish the chirping of forest crickets, the rustling needles, and the distant breathing of large megafauna prowling through the shadows.

Beneath the primordial stars, the bond between the modern engineer and the stone-age huntress deepened through firelight and shared understanding, bridging the chasm of ten thousand years.

Remembering the compact parcel he had tucked into his pack from Zone Blue before departing Fortress Alpha, Thame retrieved it from his gear.

Label code `#TH-102948`—a sturdy, cylindrical cardboard box, reassuringly dense in the hand.

Using his 1095 carbon steel bushcraft knife, Thame sliced through the packing tape in the dim firelight.

Inside lay a piece of high-precision optical equipment: a 12x50 Monocular Telescope, its multi-coated FMC glass gleaming emerald green in the dark.

Encased in rugged, non-slip black rubber armor, it was IPX7 waterproof and equipped with an integrated lens compass and an emergency mini-LED light.

Thame raised the optic to his eye, sweeping across the limestone ridge ahead; the twelve-power magnification pulled cliffs and pine crowns miles away into razor-sharp clarity.

Adjusting the knurled focus ring, the 50mm objective lens captured ambient starlight with remarkable brightness.

Handing the optic to Elya, she held it to her right eye with hesitant uncertainty at first.

Yet the instant the distant mountain terrain expanded sharply across her field of view, the huntress gasped and recoiled in shock, before eagerly pressing the lens back to her eye in utter astonishment.

"Eyes of the giant eagle... seeing far beyond the mountains!" Elya whispered in trembling wonder, caressing the rubber chassis.

Thame took the monocular back to scout the ridge trail they would navigate at dawn.

Yet as he swept the optic past the next jagged limestone ridge, peering deep into a shadowed chasm four kilometers distant...

A faint, unnatural amber flicker reflected through the darkness, accompanied by a twisting spiral of gray smoke rising into the midnight sky!

It was no wildfire, nor the reflection of stars... someone was tending a campfire deep within that forbidden gorge.

At that very moment, a ferocious subzero gale screamed down through the chasm, the bone-chilling draft causing the campfire flames beneath their rocky shelter to violently flicker in grim warning.

A midnight smoke beacon, and a blizzard wind gathering strength with terrifying speed... what lay waiting for them in the mountain pass ahead?
